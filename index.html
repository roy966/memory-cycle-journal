AMD Says Demand Beats Supply, but the Midterm Count Knocks Memory Stocks Back

AMD’s results backed the case for tight memory into 2027. Then early US election results, including a Texas governor who backs pausing data-center approvals, turned Seoul and Tokyo lower.

Kioxia’s low is against Tuesday’s ¥11,345 close; it was about −8% late in the session (MONEY BOX). Correction to No. 28: Tuesday’s closes were Samsung −4.15% and SK hynix −5.03% (Yonhap). Sources: Financial News, Hankyung, Yonhap, MONEY BOX, AMD, TrendForce, market quote (won).

AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45%, with data-center sales up 97% to $8.5 billion, and guided the fourth quarter to about $17 billion. It expects revenue to grow more than 80% in 2027. Chief executive Lisa Su said “demand is higher than supply” and that AMD has secured “wafers, substrates, advanced packaging, the memory” it needs, under long-term agreements with suppliers and with customers, where costs are “shared”. Micron rose 2.58% and Sandisk 4.78% on Tuesday before the report (AMD, CNBC, call transcript).

Overnight, the mood changed. Democrats were projected to win the House, and possibly the Senate. In Texas, AP projected Hinojosa as governor; she supports a moratorium on new data centers. Nasdaq-100 futures fell about 0.8% and CoreWeave about 7% (CNBC). Seoul opened 2.43% higher on AMD, then turned lower as foreigners sold ₩2.5 trillion of shares, and Samsung and SK hynix fell 2–3% by 2 p.m. (Financial News, Yonhap). In Tokyo, Kioxia fell as much as 15% intraday, its second straight sharp drop (MONEY BOX).

Our call: unchanged overall; DRAM steady with firmer demand signals, NAND steady. Cycle signal: fundamentals intact; the new risk is US policy on data-center timing

Demand · Customers · PCs

AMD Has Locked In Its Memory. PC Buyers Are Paying for It

AMD said PC sell-through slowed “as higher memory and component costs weighed on demand”. Client revenue fell 4% from the second quarter, and AMD plans for 2027 PC units to fall by double digits. Gross margin was 56%, guided to about 55%, and inventory rose to $11.5 billion. AMD expects data center to be more than 80% of revenue in 2027. Its comments fit a pattern: big AI buyers sign long contracts and share the cost, while consumer devices absorb the price rises (AMD, call transcript, CNBC).

Policy · Data centers

Texas Vote Puts the Largest Planned Data-Center Market on Pause Watch

Wells Fargo says Texas accounts for 27% of planned US data-center capacity in gigawatts, and its strategist called the result a “left-tail event” for AI stocks (CNBC). A moratorium would need the state legislature, so nothing has stopped yet. For memory, the risk is timing: projects for 2027–28 could slip, while chips for this year and next are already under contract. Opinion Investor Brad Gerstner says 10–15 GW of the 43 GW expected online in 2027 will not arrive (via X).

Supply · Taiwan · Korea

Winbond Commits NT$73 Billion to a New DRAM Fab as Nanya Posts a 12th Straight Record

Winbond’s board approved NT$73.163 billion for Kaohsiung Module B: 50,000–60,000 wafers a month at 16nm, ground-breaking in late December, first small output in the second half of 2029 and full output in 2030. President Chen Pei-ming said supply stays tight in 2027–28, some tool lead times exceed two years, and long-term deals with big customers cover 2027–2030. Fourth-quarter prices still rise, but by less than in the third quarter (Commercial Times). Nanya’s October revenue was NT$46.48 billion, up 3.07% from September and 487.73% from a year earlier (TechNews). Reuters reports that the US is holding up tool licences for SK hynix’s Dalian NAND expansion, while its Solidigm unit weighs about $45 billion for two fabs in New York (Investing.com, Aju). Separately, Samsung’s second Taylor fab will make 2nm HBM base dies for AMD and Nvidia from 2030, Maeil Business reports; Samsung declined to comment (CNA) Report.

Verdict vs previous call (No. 28, Nov 3)
Late upcycle; tight through 2027 and booked into 2028. Unchanged overall. DRAM is steady, and demand signals firmed: AMD says demand exceeds supply and has its memory under long-term contracts, Winbond sees tight supply in 2027–28, Nanya set another record, and Citi still sees a 2027 shortfall, though a smaller one. NAND is steady after two looser days. The new risk is political: Texas could delay data-center approvals, which would hit 2027–28 timing and valuations before it hits contract prices. Spot is flat, not falling. Playbook: margins are in the peak zone, and new fabs keep being announced, but their output lands in 2029–30. Next tests: Qualcomm and Arm tonight, and Nvidia on Nov 17.
