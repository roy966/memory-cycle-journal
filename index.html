TrendForce Puts a Date on the DRAM Price Turn as Memory Stocks Bounce

In its base case, contract-price gains narrow through 2027, level off in the second half and turn down from early 2028. The bear case starts the decline in late 2027. Memory stocks rallied anyway in Friday’s half session: Sandisk +16%, Micron +7.6%, SOX +4.7%.

TrendForce has now given dates for the turn. Its Nov 27 release says DRAM buying has slowed slightly since early 4Q26 as the supply chain reworks its 2027–28 plans. It gives three reasons: the midterms could slow US data-center building, Nvidia may change how it finances neoclouds, and new BIS rules limit chips for data centers serving Chinese firms. In the base case, conventional DRAM contract-price gains narrow through 2027, may level off in 2H27, and turn into declines across all product types from 1Q28 as bit supply keeps growing. In the bear case, OEM order cuts, destocking and neocloud consolidation start the declines in 4Q27 (TrendForce). TrendForce called these scenarios, not a forecast. It gave no size for the 2028 declines and no view on HBM.

Stocks rose regardless. In the half session, Sandisk rose 15.96% to $85.36, Micron 7.60% to $646.95 and Western Digital 7.22%. The SOX gained 4.69% to 9,186.71 and Nvidia only 0.80% (StockAnalysis, Nasdaq). Investing.com credited oversold technicals and Kioxia’s Nvidia SSD test; XTB told Reuters the bounce “seems technical” (via Stocktwits). In Tokyo, Kioxia rose 8.86% to ¥8,450, helped by about ¥1.1 trillion of TOPIX index buying (GuruFocus, citing Daiwa). In Seoul, the KOSPI closed 4.34% higher at 4,915.68; SK hynix rose 5.41% and Samsung 4.55% (Yonhap).

Spot slipped: DDR5 16Gb −0.71% to $60.333 after a flat week, DDR4 16Gb −1.13% (TrendForce). DDR5 spot is still up 126.9% from a year ago (MoneyToday, citing DRAMeXchange).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 in servers and HBM, a touch looser. TrendForce has put dates on the turn, and DDR5 spot has started to slip. On the other side, Micron says customers are getting half the DRAM they ask for. Cycle signal: turn dated to 2028, spot slipping

Micron · Supply · Japan

Micron Japan: Customers Get About Half the DRAM They Ask For

Kota Nosaka, president of Micron Memory Japan, told Nikkei that customers are receiving only about 50% of the DRAM they request. He said AI demand will make supply even tighter in 2027 and 2028, and that Micron’s 2027 supply is essentially sold out (Nikkei, EconoTimes). Equipment moves into the new Hiroshima fab in 2H28, and capacity will be added in phases after that, so Hiroshima brings no new supply before 2029. That fits CEO Sanjay Mehrotra’s statement that more than 75% of Micron’s 2027 output is committed. It is a supplier speaking, though, and it does not match TrendForce’s report of slower buying. Micron reports FQ1 on Dec 17.

SK hynix · Shareholder returns

SK hynix Doubles Its Daily Buyback Orders After the Slide

In a Friday filing, SK hynix said it will place buyback orders for about 700,000 shares a day, up from about 350,000, because it believes the shares are increasingly undervalued. It plans further buybacks through new board resolutions once the current program ends (Korea Herald, Seoul Economic Daily). The stock closed at ₩1,110,000, against an intraday high of ₩2.99 million on June 25. For fiscal 2026 the company targets about ₩100 trillion of shareholder returns: at least ₩30 trillion of dividends and more than ₩60 trillion of buybacks and cancellations (Korea Herald). Investing.com says Micron’s buyback window opens Dec 9. Buybacks show that management sees value at these prices. They say nothing about when the cycle turns.

Nvidia · Export controls · HBM buyers

Blackwell Racks Found at a Shanghai Site; Google Weighs Buying HBM Directly

Bloomberg found GB300 NVL72 and GB200 racks in loan and regulatory filings for the Yangtze River Delta Project in Shanghai. How they reached China is unknown (via Engadget). Reported Jensen Huang testifies to Congress on Dec 3. Tighter enforcement would put more server demand at risk, which is the BIS factor in TrendForce’s bear case. Meanwhile Google is reportedly exploring buying HBM for its TPUs directly instead of through Broadcom (DigiTimes via Asia Business Daily). Counterpoint estimates the broker layer adds 15–20% to HBM costs, and that HBM bit demand for AI ASICs rises 35x from 2024 to 2028. Samsung and SK hynix would gain a direct customer that has more pricing power.
