Korea’s Chip Exports Hit $56.4 Billion in October as DRAM Prices Keep Rising

Semiconductor shipments more than tripled from a year earlier and topped $50 billion for a second month, though they came in below September’s record. The ministry’s DRAM price series rose again; its NAND price was flat for a second month.

What this shows: Korea’s memory exports are still running at record levels, and DRAM prices are still climbing, but NAND benchmark prices have stopped rising. Markets were shut for the weekend, so all share prices are Friday Oct 30 closes. Sources: Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources (MOTIR), Yonhap, Asia Economy, Reuters; DRAMeXchange via BusinessKorea; prices from the Oct 31 edition (Yahoo Finance, TrendForce).

Korea’s exports rose 93.7% from a year earlier to $115.28 billion in October, close to the +93.2% a Reuters poll expected, and the trade surplus was $42.5 billion (Yonhap, Reuters). Chips made up nearly half: $56.44 billion, up 259.4%, the second month above $50 billion but below September’s record $60.3 billion (MOTIR, Asia Economy). Computer exports, driven by server SSDs, rose 496.6% to $5.85 billion.

The ministry’s fixed-price series shows where the strength is. DDR5 16Gb rose to $50.0 from $48.0 in September, its fourth straight monthly rise from $40.0 in June. The 128Gb NAND price was $30.6 for a second month, after $28.8 in June (MOTIR).

Our call: unchanged overall. DRAM is still tightening; NAND price growth has stalled. Cycle signal: DRAM firm, NAND flat

Prices · Contract

DRAM Contract Price Sets a Record While NAND’s 21-Month Climb Stalls

DRAMeXchange put October’s DDR4 8Gb PC DRAM contract price at a record $27.50, up 5.77% from $26.00 in September. The 128Gb MLC NAND benchmark held at $30.61, ending 21 straight monthly rises (BusinessKorea, Osegi). TrendForce says MLC buyers are switching to relatively cheaper SLC or TLC parts, and sees MLC easing slightly in the fourth quarter, while SLC rose 5–10% in October. It kept its forecast of a 13–18% rise in fourth-quarter PC DRAM contract prices expects November prices slightly above October’s, and sees Korean makers’ PC DRAM output falling more than 20% next year.

Customers · OpenAI

Altman Meets Samsung and SK Leaders as the Memory Crunch Deepens

OpenAI’s Sam Altman met about 50 Samsung executives in Yongin on Saturday, building on the deal for Samsung to supply memory for the Stargate project (Bloomberg via The Straits Times, Seoul Economic Daily). SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won hosted him for lunch the same day, with SK hynix and SK Telecom executives present; local press expect HBM supply and AI data centers to have been discussed (Yonhap, Financial News). No new supply deal was announced. When the letters of intent were signed a year ago, OpenAI said the two makers were targeting up to 900,000 DRAM wafers a month.

Supply · China

CXMT Says Its Next-Generation 4F² DRAM Has Passed Sample Validation

China’s CXMT said its sixth-generation DRAM platform, built on a 4F² vertical-channel cell, has completed engineering-sample validation. Customer qualification comes next, and it plans to send DDR5 server-module (RDIMM) samples by the end of 2026 (Global Times, Yonhap). CXMT says it is first to reach this stage; Samsung aims to mass-produce 4F² DRAM by 2028. No yields or volumes were given. Nine-month revenue rose 738.5% to 269.02 billion yuan.
