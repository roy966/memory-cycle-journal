Nvidia Locks In Years of Memory as Its Supply Commitments Jump $111 Billion

Nvidia’s first report since Jensen Huang’s exit beat estimates and raised next year’s growth outlook to about 75%. Its purchase commitments rose to $390 billion, mainly on multi-year memory deals. Memory stocks bounced after hours and in Seoul, though the gains faded by midday.

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion on Tuesday, up 103% from a year earlier and well above its own $108 billion guide. It guided the January quarter, which has an extra week, to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, against a consensus of about $125 billion, with no data-center compute revenue from China. Interim chief executive Colette Kress raised the outlook for fiscal 2028 revenue growth to about 75% from 70%. Vera Rubin, which pairs HBM4 with LPDDR5X, brought in nearly $20 billion in its first quarter of shipments.

For memory the key figure was in the supply chain. Kress said purchase commitments rose to $390 billion from $279 billion. Nvidia’s filing attributes the increase mainly to multi-year procurement of memory supply, Seoul Economic Daily and TrendForce reported. “We’ve secured memory, we’ve secured advanced packaging,” she told analysts, and added that “demand far exceeds supply.” Memory costs show up in its margins: gross margin falls to a guided 71–72% in the January quarter, which Kress called the bottom, because Nvidia is still filling orders at earlier prices, then recovers to 72–73% in fiscal 2028.

Memory shares had drifted lower on Tuesday: Micron fell 0.92% to $712.42 and Sandisk 1.47% to $797.24, while the SOX closed up 0.34% at 9,642.88. After the results Micron rose 4.69% to $745.80 in after-hours trading and SK hynix’s US shares 5.71% to $129.61, Benzinga reported, and Sandisk gained about 4–5%. In Seoul the KOSPI opened 2.62% higher at 5,098.82, with Samsung up 4.1% and SK hynix up 5.7% early, Yonhap reported. The gains faded by midday (Samsung +1.10%, SK hynix +3.83% at 13:07 KST, eToday); at 14:50 KST the index was up 1.83% at 5,059.25 as foreigners sold a net ₩380 billion, Korea Economic Daily reported.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027. A touch tighter: the largest buyer of HBM has committed to years of memory purchases, and TrendForce now expects DRAM prices to keep rising through 2027. NAND and 2028 remain the weak spots. Cycle signal: demand confirmed, memory volume locked in for years

DRAM · HBM · NAND · Forecasts

TrendForce Sees DRAM Prices Rising Every Quarter of 2027 and HBM4 Prices More Than Doubling

At its Seoul roadshow on Wednesday, TrendForce forecast conventional DRAM average selling prices up 10–15% quarter on quarter in 4Q26. It then sees slower gains in 2027: 5–10% in the first quarter, 3–8% in the second and 0–5% in each of the last two, ZDNet Korea reported. “DRAM prices will not turn downward until next year,” senior analyst Eli Wang said, meaning 2027. NAND is different. Because YMTC is expanding aggressively, NAND prices turn down from the second quarter of 2027. HBM4 pricing goes from at least $2.05 per gigabit this year to $3.00–5.50 in 2027, more than double, and HBM4E talks are at $5 or more. The risk is 2028, when large amounts of new capacity arrive. Wang pointed to Samsung’s P5 phases 1 and 2 at about 200,000 wafers a month each, SK hynix’s target of 300,000 a month at Yongin, and CXMT’s plan to add 100,000–200,000 a month after 2028. Its weekly note adds that mobile DRAM contract talks have stalled.

Contracts · DRAM

Apple Moves to Multi-Year DRAM Deals With Samsung and SK Hynix

Apple and Samsung are finalizing a four- to five-year DRAM supply agreement that would start in the first half of 2027, Maeil Business Newspaper reported, as relayed by TrendForce. The deal sets a minimum volume, while prices follow market conditions. Apple has reportedly also signed a long-term agreement with SK hynix on terms favorable to the supplier, and a NAND agreement possibly with Kioxia (unconfirmed). Elsewhere, China’s CXMT said on its third-quarter call that the DRAM shortage will continue through the fourth quarter as AI-server DDR5 stays tight. Verdict vs previous call (No. 42, Nov 17)
Late upcycle; tight through 2027. A touch tighter. Nvidia passed the test: it raised its outlook and committed to years of memory purchases, so HBM and DRAM volume is contracted through 2027. TrendForce now sees DRAM prices rising in every quarter of 2027, more slowly, and HBM4 prices more than doubling. Apple is signing multi-year agreements too. The playbook warning has not spread to DRAM: DDR5 spot is flat while contracts rise. NAND remains the weak link (falling from 2Q27). 2028 is still open, with new capacity arriving and SemiAnalysis’s cut to 2028 HBM unchanged.
