Korea’s Chip Exports Nearly Quadruple to a Record, but Seoul Shares Keep Sliding

Official data for the first 20 days of November confirmed what unofficial tallies had signaled: chip shipments hit a record. Investors sold anyway, and Seoul lagged a rebound in Taiwan and in US futures.

South Korea exported $38.5 billion of semiconductors in the first 20 days of November, up 294.4% from a year earlier and the highest for that stretch of any month, the Korea Customs Service said on Monday. Chips made up 49.7% of all exports, 24.3 percentage points more than a year ago. Total exports roughly doubled, up 101.5% to $77.4 billion, the best first-20-day figure on record; daily average exports rose 95.2% to $4.84 billion. Exports to China rose 145.6% and to the US 142.7%. The headline release does not split out memory. Unofficial tallies that circulated on Saturday put DRAM including modules at $23.38 billion for the period, a figure the official data neither confirms nor contradicts. Confirmed

The market looked past it. The KOSPI opened 2.77% lower and fell as much as 5.86% before lunch, triggering a five-minute sell-side “sidecar” on program trading, Aju Press and Yonhap reported. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix were each down nearly 6% at the low. Institutions bought on the way down as the government prepared a ₩10.76 trillion stock-stabilization fund, the Seoul Economic Daily said, and the index closed at 4,708.51, down 3.32%; Samsung ended at ₩177,500 (−3.06%) and SK hynix at ₩1,085,000 (−2.52%). Seoul still lagged its neighbors. Taiwan’s TAIEX rose 2.16% to 36,811.89 on the first day of its ban on short sales below the previous close, as the state stabilization fund bought shares, and Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 2.9% in Asian trading after the index’s worst week since March 2020.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027, unchanged. Record chip exports and server makers repricing signed orders (below) say 2027 supply is as tight as ever. The debate is about 2028, where Goldman has now joined BNK in modeling price declines. Cycle signal: 2027 firm, 2028 softening in forecasts

Sell-side · Samsung · SK hynix · HBM

Goldman Cuts Its 2028 Memory Price Forecasts but Says the Stocks Already Price Worse

Goldman Sachs now expects conventional DRAM prices to fall 19% in 2028, from a 5% decline before, after a 32% rise in 2027, and NAND prices to fall 21% after a 20% rise, according to a report dated Nov. 22. It cited worries about the sustainability of AI capex, a muted outlook for smartphone and PC memory, and more supply from China. It raised its HBM market estimates to $60 billion for 2026, $200 billion for 2027 and $237 billion for 2028, mainly on higher prices. Price targets fell to ₩350,000 from ₩500,000 for Samsung and to ₩2 million from ₩3.5 million for SK hynix; both stay at Buy. The bank says current share prices imply zero AI-capex growth in 2028 plus a further 40% fall in prices from its base case. Customers would have little economic reason to walk away from long-term agreements unless DRAM fell 32–54% below its base case over two years, it estimates. Confirmed

Servers · PCs · Pass-through

Server Makers Seek Up to 45% More on Signed AI Orders as Memory Costs Bite

Dell, Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Lenovo and Supermicro are asking customers to pay up to 45% more for AI servers months after contracts were signed, to pass on soaring memory costs, Digitimes reported on Monday, citing the Financial Times. Trading firm XTX Markets is fighting a Dell request for an extra $62 million to $70 million on a $549 million order, according to the reports. The increases cover GPU servers and conventional CPU systems alike, according to the reports. Reported At the low end, makers are cutting memory instead. Asus is considering going from 16GB to 8GB in budget and midrange PCs because “it has become difficult to pass on the full increase in memory costs,” co-CEO S.Y. Hsu told Electronic Times;

Contracts · Brokers

Meritz Says Buyers Are Floating Equity Stakes and Contracts of Up to 20 Years

Several US Big Tech companies have begun proposing “ultra-long” memory agreements with purchase commitments of up to 20 years, Meritz Securities analyst Kim Sun-woo wrote on Monday, Yonhap reported. Meritz’s channel checks show buyers considering equity investments, special corporate bonds and joint ventures with memory makers. The broker said SoftBank was not the only company to discuss investing in a memory maker, and that it appears to want a role in production, not just a contract. Reported
