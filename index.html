Memory’s Week Hinges on Nvidia’s First Report Without Huang, and Seoul Is Already Picking Sides

Nvidia reports on Tuesday with the market looking for a small beat and a lot of reassurance. In Seoul, foreign investors spent last week buying Samsung and selling SK hynix, the supplier seen as more exposed to Nvidia, while borrowed money in the two chip giants remains unusually high.

Nvidia reports its fiscal third quarter after the US close on Tuesday, Nov 17. Results are due at about 23:20 Israel time and the call starts at midnight. It is the first report under interim CEO Colette Kress. The company guided to $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, with a 74% gross margin, and LSEG consensus is about $109.2 billion and $2.48 a share, VLSI Korea reported. Options imply a move of 6.7% to 9.6% on the day. The brokers are split: HSBC cut the stock to hold with a $170 target, while Cantor Fitzgerald trimmed its target to $250 and stayed overweight. For memory, watch the supply commitments and how much customer financing Kress keeps.

Seoul is already pricing who is more exposed. The KOSPI closed Friday at 5,290.53, down 0.98% on the day but up 4.56% on the week, the Seoul Economic Daily reported. Foreign investors bought a net ₩1.23 trillion of Samsung Electronics and sold a net ₩1.05 trillion of SK hynix. Samsung rose 2.23% on the week and SK hynix fell 3.80%. The paper says SK hynix “may be relatively more sensitive, given the large share of its high-bandwidth memory that goes to Nvidia.” Shinyoung Securities puts the KOSPI at 3.7 times forward earnings, against a 10-year average of 10.2.

Leverage adds to the swings. Margin loans in Samsung and SK hynix total nearly ₩7 trillion, 27.2% of all margin debt on the KOSPI and KOSDAQ as of Nov 12, according to FnGuide data cited by Aju Press. As of Nov 13 the KOSPI was 42% below its June 22 peak of 9,114.55, with Samsung down 41.7% and SK hynix 58.3% from their highs.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall. No new prices this weekend; conventional DRAM a touch tighter on Taiwan’s outlook. Cycle signal: fundamentals steady, waiting on Nvidia

DRAM · Taiwan

Taiwan’s Module Makers Brace for a Tighter DRAM First Half as Nanya Posts an 85.7% Gross Margin

Taiwan’s memory module makers expect DRAM shortages in the first half of 2027 to be worse than this year, as chipmakers give AI applications first call on capacity, Central News Agency reported on Sunday (via Economic Daily News). Apacer said it is holding stock back for 2027. The chipmakers posted record quarters: Nanya Technology’s gross margin reached 85.7% (EPS NT$25.4), Winbond 70.57% and Macronix 70.49%. Module makers are being squeezed. Transcend’s gross margin fell from 77.03% to 56.5% and Apacer’s from 51.25% to 40.08%. They see NAND supply and demand as “relatively balanced.” TeamGroup CEO Gerry Chen said earlier that the worst shortage would run from 4Q26 to the end of 2Q27, with balance in 2H28 at the earliest (Wccftech, Nov 14).

HBM · Customers

Korea’s Chipmakers Step Up the Hunt for Customers Beyond Nvidia

Samsung and SK hynix are racing to sign big customers other than Nvidia as AMD, Broadcom, Google and Amazon push their own accelerators, CWN reported on Sunday. Samsung is drawing on its July agreement with Broadcom, a five-year, $200 billion memory-supply and foundry deal, and its March designation as AMD’s priority HBM4 supplier. Nvidia made up about 25% of SK hynix’s revenue last year, which puts SK hynix closer to the leadership change. The company has long-term agreements with 14 major customers covering more than half of its 2027–28 output, and its 2027 Nvidia supply is already contracted. “The mere possibility of a reshuffle in the AI-chip supply chain does not reduce chip demand,” said Mirae Asset’s Kim Young-gun. TrendForce noted that for Taiwan’s AI suppliers the risk is timing, not orders: an industry report says delayed high-end upgrades could push some 2027 profit growth into 2028.
