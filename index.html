Micron and Sandisk Plunge 14% and 16% as the Chip Rout Hits Wall Street

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 10.7% in a day. Memory shares now trade at three to four times forward earnings, a level analysts call a warning sign rather than a bargain. Seoul, already down 5% on Friday, trades next on Monday.

Friday was the worst day of the AI selloff so far. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index opened at 9,160.80 and closed down 10.73% at 8,408.59. Micron fell 13.89% to $594.51 after trading as low as $585.25. Sandisk, in its first session after its 8-for-1 split, fell 16.05% to $79.24, and Nvidia lost 14.01% to $125.53. Western Digital and Seagate fell about 12%. The S&P 500 lost 3.7%, Kabutowire reported. StockAnalysis tied the memory losses partly to TrendForce’s forecast that NAND contract prices will start falling in the first quarter. Broadcom fell 11% after analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said Google had cut its 2027 orders for the Broadcom-designed TPU 8i by about 1 million units, roughly 20%, and would ship more of the MediaTek-designed TPU 8t instead. Kuo said the details are not final. Reported

Micron now trades at 3.76 times forward earnings, against a two-year average of 9.10, and Sandisk at 3.04 times, MarketWatch reported. Micron is down 50.99% from its peak and Sandisk 72.85%. Analysts warned against reading that as cheap. “Investors have learned over decades to sell memory stocks at peak earnings,” said Brendan Burke of the Futurum Group, who called the low multiples “a warning label, not a bargain sign.” He added that HBM and data-center SSDs may make earnings “more durable than these multiples imply.” CreditSights’ Andy Li noted that monthly options expired on Friday. Seoul had already closed before New York opened: the KOSPI fell 5.21% to 4,869.96, its lowest close since Jan. 20, with Samsung down 7.01% and SK hynix down 5.92%. Foreigners sold a net ₩1.13 trillion and institutions ₩951 billion, Yonhap reported.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027, a touch looser. Contract books still look full, and DRAM spot was flat on Friday. Google’s reported TPU cut is the first concrete sign of 2027 accelerator orders being trimmed, and that would feed through to HBM. Cycle signal: stocks pricing a peak; contracts not yet

SK hynix · Deals · Kioxia

SoftBank’s Son Explored a $1 Trillion Buyout of SK Hynix, Report Says

SoftBank chief executive Masayoshi Son recently approached Wall Street institutions about financing a possible trillion-dollar buyout of SK hynix, The Information reported, citing people familiar with the matter. A SoftBank spokesperson said the deal “is no longer under active consideration.” SK hynix said it had no knowledge of the matter and that any foreign acquisition of the company would need prior government approval under Korean law, Reuters reported via CNA. Seoul Economic Daily put SK hynix’s market value at about $577.7 billion and said the $1 trillion figure reflected its peak value plus a 40% control premium. Reported Separately, SK hynix said on Friday it will convert ¥129 billion of convertible bonds into shares, giving it an indirect 14.1% stake in Kioxia and making it the NAND maker’s largest indirect shareholder. The company said it has no plans to buy more Kioxia shares or to sell, Yonhap reported. Confirmed

Contracts · Inventory · Supply

Samsung Said to Be Nearly Sold Out Through 2029 as a Korean Broker Warns of a 2027 Inventory Correction

Most of Samsung’s 2027–2029 memory capacity is already under contract, and some customers are seeking long-term agreements that run to 2031, Economy Tribune reported on Saturday, citing a person familiar with the matter. The report says conventional DRAM wafer capacity for 2027 is effectively sold out, DRAM bit supply growth will be capped at 20–25% a year, and suppliers are meeting only 60–70% of demand. An industry official said contract demand for NAND is rising even as spot falls, and that Kioxia had raised its 2027 NAND demand forecast. Reported BNK Investment & Securities took the other side. It cut its SK hynix target by 36% to ₩1.05 million, below Friday’s close, kept a Hold rating and cut its 2027 operating-profit estimate by 12%, Hankyung reported. BNK’s Lee Min-hee expects customers and distributors to start working down inventory in the second or third quarter of 2027, with a “dramatic” fall in memory prices. He estimates DRAM bit demand rose 24% this year while supplier shipments rose 38%, the most since 2016, a sign of excess stock building in the channel. He sees global DRAM capacity rising 50% in three years and doubling in five.
