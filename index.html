Korea’s Chipmakers Are Sold Out on Paper, but Their Filings Say the Contracts Can Change

After a week that halved memory shares from their peaks, Korean press spent the weekend asking how firm the “sold out through 2029” claims really are. The filings say volumes and delivery dates can move; analysts point to price floors, deposits and penalties.

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix posted record third-quarter results, yet their shares are about half their peaks, and the JoongAng Ilbo used the weekend to test the three fears behind the selloff. Samsung’s memory sold at about 3.7 times last year’s average price in the first nine months, the paper said, citing the companies’ quarterly reports released on Nov. 16. The customer mix is widening. SK hynix’s largest customer, presumed to be Nvidia, bought ₩34.98 trillion of chips in the first nine months, but that was 14.8% of sales against 27% a year earlier, and a second customer now accounts for 10.1%. Alphabet and Amazon appear among Samsung’s five biggest customers; Nvidia does not. KB Securities’ Kim Dong-won expects Nvidia to take 38% of global HBM demand in 2027, Google 37% and AMD 13%.

The harder question is the contracts. Samsung says its 2027 HBM is sold out, and SK hynix says long-term agreements cover more than 50% of its 2027–2028 capacity. Neither quarterly report discloses terms, and both say contract volumes and delivery periods can change with customer demand, the JoongAng noted; Nvidia’s own SEC filing says some supply commitments can be cancelled or rescheduled before formal orders. Market Times added that SK hynix’s view that its DRAM and HBM output is effectively sold through 2029 includes volumes still under negotiation, while Samsung’s 2027 HBM is priced. Micron’s 10-K puts its remaining performance obligations at about $134 billion as of Sept. 3, counting only committed volumes at minimum prices. Defenders point to price floors, prepayments, penalties and the risk of losing one’s place in line. “There are no warning signs at all of a change in demand right now,” said Roh Geun-chang of Semicon Research Lab. Yuanta estimated on Nov. 6 that about 75% of the 33.8 GW of US data centers due for completion in 2027 in 20 major states is already under construction; it is 2028 builds that are exposed to permitting curbs. Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027, unchanged from Saturday’s “a touch looser.” Nothing in the weekend reporting changes the supply picture, and no new prices printed. The debate has moved from whether memory is sold out to how binding the contracts are, and that will only be settled by 2028 orders. Cycle signal: contract durability is now the question

Markets · Policy · Taiwan · Nvidia

Taiwan Bans Short Sales Below the Close After Its Worst Week in Three Decades

Taiwan’s Financial Supervisory Commission on Saturday ordered a full ban, from Monday, on short-selling stocks below the previous day’s close and suspended applications to issue call warrants, Reuters reported. Lenders were asked to stop lending out shares and to recall or not renew existing loans. The finance ministry said its stock stabilization fund had already entered the market and that foreign selling was tapering off. Taiwan stocks fell more than 6% on Friday, capping their worst week in more than 30 years. Confirmed Pressure on the chip complex’s biggest HBM buyer also grew. The Securities and Exchange Commission’s inquiry into Nvidia’s accounting is at an early stage and could end without charges, The New York Times reported on Saturday, according to AFP. Questions have focused on Nvidia’s switch to reporting sales by customer headquarters, which removed Singapore from its geographic breakdown. Nvidia fell 14% on Friday and more than 20% on the week. President Trump said he would like to see Jensen Huang “given a second chance”; chairman Harvey Jones has ruled out his return. Reported

Samsung · SK hynix · Shareholder returns

Samsung and SK Hynix Payout Ratios Jump on Record Memory Profits

Samsung’s consolidated dividend payout ratio rose to 18% at the end of September from 4.1% at the end of the first half, and SK hynix’s to 8.4% from 0.4%, Yonhap reported on Sunday from the companies’ quarterly reports. Samsung will pay ₩30 trillion, or ₩4,601 a share, for the third quarter, against ₩372 in the first quarter. SK hynix will pay ₩20,000 a share, or ₩14 trillion, up from ₩375 in each of the first two quarters. Nine-month net profit was ₩193.6 trillion at Samsung and ₩174 trillion at SK hynix, against ₩25.57 trillion and ₩27.7 trillion a year earlier.
