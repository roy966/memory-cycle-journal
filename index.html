Memory Stocks Plunge in Seoul as the AI Selloff Spreads, but Brokers Say Contract Prices Are Still Rising

Samsung and SK hynix fell more than 6% and the KOSPI tripped a sell-sidecar after the SOX lost 2.4%. KB Securities and Mirae Asset say the shares are tracking the spot market, not the contract prices behind earnings.

Anthropic’s record listing was supposed to lift the AI trade. Instead, its shares closed at $97.17, up 17% from the $83 offer price but well below the $115 high, and all three main US indexes fell at least 2%, Reuters reported. The S&P 500 lost 2.02% for a sixth straight decline. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index opened at 9,938.33 and closed down 2.41% at 9,419.72. Micron opened at $740.33 and closed down 2.10% at $690.44. Sandisk fell 2.71% to $755.16 in its last session before the 8-for-1 split, and Nvidia lost 3.30% to $145.98, a sixth straight loss, after touching a 52-week low. Separately, TSMC and Samsung paused some chip shipments to Chinese customers, including Samsung foundry clients ByteDance and Baidu’s Kunlun, while they apply for licenses under a new US rule, the Financial Times reported.

Seoul took the full hit on Friday. The KOSPI opened 4.08% lower, and a sell-sidecar suspended program selling for five minutes at 11:54 after KOSPI 200 futures fell 5.04%, The Asia Business Daily reported. At 14:12 KST the index was down 5.50% at 4,855.27. Samsung Electronics was down 7.21% and SK hynix 6.68%. Foreigners had sold a net ₩1.18 trillion and institutions ₩805 billion, while individuals bought ₩1.55 trillion, fnnews reported.

Two Korean brokers argued the selling has run ahead of the industry. KB Securities said DRAM spot fell 5% and NAND spot 20% over the past month, while contract prices kept rising, leaving a gap of more than 20%. It expects Samsung and SK hynix contract prices to rise 40% for DRAM and 30% for NAND next year. It sees 2027 DRAM demand up 25% against 18% supply growth, with major customers getting only about 70% of what they need. “Market pessimism toward the two companies is close to peaking,” KB said, according to Seoul Economic Daily. Mirae Asset’s Kim Young-geon called the reaction excessive. He puts Samsung at 1.2 times and SK hynix at 1.3 times 12-month forward book value, comparable to levels from before AI, when return on equity was around 10%, against expected ROE above 50% over the next three years. He added that some Greater China customers have deferred memory contracts because of GPU procurement limits, Edaily reported.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 for DRAM and HBM. Unchanged. DRAM spot was flat this morning and contract books look firm. The stocks moved on AI-capex and China fears, plus a weaker NAND outlook. Cycle signal: contracts firm, shares capitulating

NAND · Contracts · Supply

TrendForce Sees NAND Contract Prices Falling From the First Quarter as Chinese Clouds Cut SSD Orders

TrendForce cut its fourth-quarter NAND contract forecast to gains of 8–13% on Thursday. It now expects prices to fall 0–5% quarter on quarter in the first quarter of 2027, with every product category in decline from the second quarter. Chinese cloud providers, short of GPUs, holding high inventories and fighting over token prices, have cut their 2027 enterprise SSD orders by more than US cloud providers have raised theirs. TrendForce sees 2027 bit supply up 25–30% and industry capex up 26.7% to $29.9 billion, and notes spot prices have already corrected by more than 20%. Sandisk offered the counterpoint at a Morgan Stanley conference late Wednesday, according to Investing.com’s transcript. Data-center NAND revenue rose 1,700% from a year earlier to nearly half of sales. Eight long-term hyperscaler contracts carry a minimum 80% gross margin and should make up about two-thirds of fiscal 2028 revenue. Customers now come to it offering three-year contracts, Goeckeler said. Demand for durable SLC flash could add “tens of exabytes,” and if 1–1.5 points of supply went to SLC, 20% supply growth would fall to about 16%. Phison chief executive K.S. Pua blamed China’s enterprise SSD slowdown on GPU shortages and said big cloud providers would keep expanding, DigiTimes reported. Reported

Samsung · Leadership · Calendar

Samsung Hands Memory to Its HBM Chief

Samsung promoted Hwang Sang-joon to president and named him head of the memory business, succeeding Vice Chairman Jun Young-hyun, the Korea Herald reported. Hwang led DRAM and HBM development; Jun stays co-CEO and will focus on foundry.
