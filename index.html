Buyers Offer Prepayments and Fab Financing for Memory as Seoul’s Chip Stocks Rebound

Citi says customers want binding contracts of up to five years and some pay 25–30% upfront. Musk reportedly offered to guarantee financing for a Samsung fab and was told everyone makes that offer. SK hynix and Samsung gained about 5% and 4% as foreign buyers came back.

The buyers’ side of the shortage came into view. Peter Lee, Citigroup’s semiconductor analyst, said suppliers and customers increasingly want binding long-term contracts of up to five years; customers are paying 25% to 30% of the purchase price upfront, and some are sharing the cost of new fabs, the Wall Street Journal reported (via Businesskorea). The Journal also relayed an account from Capital Group’s Carl Kawaja: Elon Musk offered Samsung a long-term purchase deal and then a financing guarantee for a new fab, and both times Chairman Lee Jae-yong replied that everyone else was making the same offer. Reported That answers Wednesday’s doubts (Yonhap Infomax) about how binding the LTAs are.

Demand forecasts are still rising even as price forecasts come down. Goldman’s Nov 23 model, recirculated on X overnight, lifts industry DRAM demand growth to +33%/+24%/+17% for 2026–28 (from +28%/+20%/+19%), with server DRAM excluding HBM +32% in 2027 and SOCAMM +75%. It also raises supply growth to +29%/+23%/+22%, so the shortage narrows to 1.2% in 2028; that is the basis for its price cuts (Longbridge, BigGo). BofA argues that even a 40–50% fall in 2028 prices would leave DRAM operating margins at 50–60%, against 80%+ now, because of HBM and LTAs (as quoted by @pequityresearch; Single source).

Seoul traded the news. The KOSPI opened lower, then rallied to close at 4,907.11 (+4.16%) as foreign investors bought and the market priced an expanded stabilization fund (Seoul Economic Daily, Edaily). SK hynix closed at ₩1,107,000 (+5.13%) and Samsung at ₩178,800 (+4.38%) on the day Samsung pays out about ₩30 trillion of dividends (Edaily). In Tokyo on Thursday, Kioxia hit its 14.5% daily limit and closed at ¥7,762 (+12.57%) after its holding company disclosed a ¥2.21 trillion dividend from its operating unit, which Citi said makes a buyback possible this year (Startup Fortune, Bloomberg).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 in servers and HBM, unchanged overall. Contract terms and demand look firmer today, but the 2027 price path is flatter (see On X), and stranded GPUs in Southeast Asia are the near-term risk to spot. Cycle signal: contracts firmer, price path flatter

Export controls · Servers · Spot risk

Nvidia Halts Server Shipments to Four Southeast Asian Countries

Nvidia told Dell, Supermicro and other partners to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times and the Wall Street Journal reported. The duration is unclear. It follows a Commerce rule, effective Nov 19, that requires licences for advanced chips there (Reuters via Investing.com, TechTimes). Reported For memory, the question is whether stranded capacity is absorbed or dumped. Goldman puts China-linked AI capacity at 0.5–0.75 GW, or 263,000–395,000 GPUs, that must find licences or new tenants within 90 days (via @pequityresearch; Single source). A Jefferies note says the first estimate cuts in the Asian server chain (Quanta, Compal, Supermicro) are “not impossible” by fourth-quarter results, adding pressure on memory spot prices. B300 lead times are 26–40 weeks (Commercial Times via Newspim), so US demand is still ahead of supply.

Micron · Taiwan · Labor

Micron’s Taoyuan Mediation Makes Progress; Union to Table Its Own Plan Dec 2

Thursday’s second mediation between Micron and its Taoyuan union ended without a deal but with progress, the Taoyuan labor bureau said: the union will give the company its own proposal on Dec 2, and a third mediation is set for 09:00 Taiwan time on Dec 4 (Commercial Times). The union says the dispute is now about a peace clause, not pay (UURL). The reported threat to NT$1.6 trillion of Micron investment remains Unconfirmed. Taiwan makes more than 60% of Micron’s DRAM (Digital Times). Talks continuing lowers the near-term disruption risk.

HBM · Flash · Technology

Samsung Faces an HBM Base-Die Crunch as Kioxia Pitches Flash as GPU Memory

Samsung’s memory division expects its need for HBM base-die wafers to double in 2027 to about 30,000 4nm-equivalent wafers a month, so Samsung plans to add base-die lines at Giheung, Pyeongtaek P5 and Taylor P2, TrendForce reported, citing industry sources. Reported Kioxia showed XL-FLASH Gen 3: 3µs average reads, under a tenth of its BiCS8 TLC, and 70% higher bit density, with samples in March 2027. In a proof of concept with Nvidia 144 Kioxia SSDs gave a GB200 NVL72 rack about 60TB of flash “extended memory” per node; no latency figures were given (Kioxia). Huawei will present its own HBM design at ISSCC, with a 4,096-bit interface and hybrid bonding (TrendForce), a possible route around China’s EUV limits.
