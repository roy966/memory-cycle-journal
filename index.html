TrendForce Trims Its 2027 DRAM Price Outlook as BNP Warns of an Earlier Memory Peak

The tracker cut its forecast for next year’s contract-price gains, and BNP Paribas downgraded Micron, saying supply and demand could reach parity by mid-2027. Micron fell 11%, and Seoul’s chip giants gave back Tuesday’s rally.

What this shows: Tuesday’s US closes from MarketWatch, Investing.com and Yonhap; SOX level from Yahoo Finance. Korea closes from Naver Finance. BNP’s call as reported by Investing.com. Spot prices are from TrendForce’s Wednesday morning session.

TrendForce said on Wednesday it had lowered its forecast for 2027 DRAM contract-price increases because the supply chain’s demand outlook is unclear. In its weekly DRAM bulletin, the tracker said Chinese cloud providers have cut some server DRAM demand, and US data-center policy and geopolitics add uncertainty. Spot buyers are cutting inventory first, and consumer DRAM demand next year “may be weaker than expected.” The free summary did not give the new figures; the call is still for smaller increases, not declines. Confirmed

BNP Paribas analyst Karl Ackerman downgraded Micron to Neutral from Outperform and cut his target to $775 from $1,700, Investing.com reported. “Growing risks to hyperscaler capacity plans in 2028 further threaten S/D fundamentals,” he wrote, citing less HBM in Nvidia’s Vera Rubin Ultra, falling PC and phone units and new wafer capacity. He sees DRAM and NAND supply and demand nearing parity from mid-2027, a year earlier than before, and notes that prices peaked on a year-over-year basis in August; “memory stocks peak when ASPs peak on a Y/Y basis.” BNP’s 2028 EPS estimate is 45% below consensus, with a trough of $86 in 2029. Micron closed at $629.79, down 11.01%, and the SOX fell 7.88%. Reported

Seoul gave back Tuesday’s gains. A sell-side sidecar was triggered at 9:08 a.m., and the KOSPI closed at 4,697.49 (−6.16%), below Monday’s close. Samsung Electronics ended at ₩170,700 and SK hynix at ₩1,030,000. Foreign investors turned net sellers again, and Yonhap said the BNP note “poured cold water” on sentiment, alongside US tariff and Fed worries. Kiwoom’s Han Ji-young called Tuesday’s jump “an early stage in the retreat of excessive pessimism” rather than a trend reversal (Aju Press).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027, but a notch looser. DRAM is no longer firmer: TrendForce cut its 2027 price path and phone makers are cutting 2027 builds. Server demand is still strong, as Dell and Nvidia’s lead times show. Cycle signal: DRAM softer, NAND loose

Earnings · Servers · PCs

Dell Sees No Server-Memory Price Declines as Its AI Backlog Hits $132 Billion

Dell reported record fiscal third-quarter revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103%, booked $60.2 billion of AI orders and ended with a $132.3 billion AI backlog. It raised its full-year revenue guidance to $202 billion from $192 billion. Chief operating officer Jeff Clarke said on the call that “there’s nothing that suggests that we’re going to see price declines in enterprise NAND and DRAM for servers,” as HBM takes wafer capacity from other memory. “We think that crunch continues clearly into ’27,” he said; inflation will slow from 2026 but the market stays inflationary. No customers have rescheduled, he said. HP, by contrast, said PC units fell 29% in its fourth quarter, with its Personal Systems margin at 3.0%, as it worked to “address memory and storage cost pressures.” Both companies warned of continued memory cost pressure (Reuters). Demand · Phones · GPUs

Phone Makers Cut 2027 Targets as Memory Costs Squeeze Consumer Devices

Major smartphone brands have lowered their 2027 shipment targets because of memory costs and shortages, Jiemian News reported, citing supply-chain sources (via Business Standard). Xiaomi is aiming for about 90 million units, down 25%; vivo 60 million (−29%); OPPO 90 million (−14%); Samsung 210 million (−9.5%); Apple 220 million (−5.6%); Huawei below 50 million. Only Honor kept its target. Reported Graphics is being squeezed too. Taiwan’s Commercial Times reported that new Nvidia B300 orders face lead times of 26–40 weeks, and that GeForce RTX 50 supply is being cut as memory makers favor server DRAM over GDDR7. Reported

HBM · Samsung · Supply

Samsung’s Need for HBM Base Dies Seen Doubling as Its HBM4E Reaches 17Gbps

Samsung’s memory division expects to need about 30,000 4nm-equivalent wafers a month of HBM base dies in 2027, double this year, TrendForce reported, citing industry sources. Samsung is said to be planning base-die lines at Giheung, part of Pyeongtaek P5 and its Taylor P2 fab in Texas. TrendForce also cited Hankyung: Samsung aims to double HBM4 and HBM4E output in 2027, lifting monthly HBM wafer input from 180,000 to 250,000. Separately, Samsung researchers will present a 48GB HBM4E running at 17Gbps per pin at ISSCC on Feb. 16, Korean media reported. Reported In Taiwan, 84.6% of Micron’s Taoyuan union members who voted (1,567 of 1,852) rejected the company’s offer. The union wants a “reasonable” proposal at talks on Thursday (Liberty Times).
