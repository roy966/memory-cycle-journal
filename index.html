Korea’s Chip Exports Quadruple in Early November as Seoul Extends Its Rebound

Semiconductor exports for Nov. 1–10 rose 331% from a year earlier to $16.6 billion, half of everything Korea shipped. SK hynix gained 5.36% on Wednesday even after a weak US session in which Nvidia fell 5.47%. DDR5 spot edged up after two flat sessions.

What this shows: Seoul ignored Wall Street’s soft session. The SK hynix ADR fell 3.51% to $129.66 on Tuesday, then the Korean shares rose 5.36% on Wednesday on the export data. DDR5 spot ticked up and DDR4 kept easing. NAND wafer spot ($16.15, −4.33% w/w) is carried from the last weekly print. US Oct CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%), above forecasts; the 10-year yield rose to 4.98%. Sources: KRX via Naver Finance, Korea Customs Service via Yonhap Infomax and Maeil Business, Yahoo Finance, CNBC, BLS, TrendForce.

Korea’s exports for the first ten days of November reached $33.5 billion, up 111.9% from a year earlier and a record for the period. Daily average exports rose 97.7% to $4.46 billion. Semiconductors brought in $16.6 billion, up 331.2%, and made up 49.7% of exports, 25.3 points more than a year ago. Computer peripherals, which include SSDs, rose 237.7%. Imports rose 45.0% to $24.7 billion, leaving a surplus of $8.8 billion. Chip export values reflect prices as well as volumes.

The KOSPI closed at 5,375.36 (+2.60%), its second straight gain after November’s sell-off; Samsung closed at ₩206,000 and SK hynix at ₩1,277,000. Preliminary exchange data on Naver show foreigners net bought about ₩655.3bn, a second day of buying; Hankyung put their November net selling at ₩10.57 trillion before the turn. In New York on Tuesday the SOX slipped 0.35% to 9,998.33. Micron fell 1.10% to $725.11 after touching $699.63, and Sandisk 2.99% to $859.99. Broadcom rose 3.91% and AMD 1.30%. Spot DRAM was mixed: DDR5 16Gb rose 0.11% to $60.233 and DDR5 eTT 0.33% to $30.40, while DDR4 16Gb slipped 0.15% to $79.867 and 8Gb 0.24% to $44.893. TrendForce’s Tuesday spot report said branded-chip trading was quiet and RDIMM module prices were correcting downward.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall. Near-term evidence is a touch firmer (record chip exports, a DDR5 spot uptick, CoreWeave’s demand comments). The 2028 debate is a touch looser (Morgan Stanley’s terminal-margin cut and new warnings about CXMT capacity). Cycle signal: demand data firm; 2028 debate opens

Brokers · Supply 2028

Morgan Stanley Cuts SK Hynix Target 38% as the Debate Moves to 2028

Morgan Stanley cut its SK hynix target price 38% to ₩1.6 million and Samsung’s 19% to ₩309,000, while raising its 2027 operating-profit forecasts by 13% and 9%, the Korea Herald reported. It lowered SK hynix’s average operating margin for 2029–35 to 28% from 53%. It moved about 4.9 GW of data-center capacity from 2028 into 2029 and expects new memory supply from late 2027, a setup it calls a 2028 earnings-cliff risk. It stays Overweight on both, with Samsung preferred. Nomura holds targets of ₩4.7 million and ₩670,000 and sees demand above supply at least through 2028. SK hynix has said long-term agreements with 14 customers cover more than half of its 2027–28 capacity. Separately, Charles Kau, a former Nanya president, told Taiwanese media that TSMC would never make DRAM. He warned that CXMT is building 10 fabs and could bring large capacity online within two years, risking a glut. He doubts its 4F² DDR5 parts will reach mass production soon (TrendForce, Nov 11).

AI demand · Nvidia

CoreWeave Raises Its Backlog as Nvidia Slides on Its CEO Probe

CoreWeave reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion, against a $3.55 billion LSEG estimate. It guided 2026 revenue to $13.0–13.3 billion and lifted its backlog to about $132.5 billion; the shares rose about 7% after hours (CNBC). Nvidia fell 5.47% to $168.89 and briefly traded below a $4 trillion market value as its board’s probe into Jensen Huang continued; a shareholder class action has been filed (CNBC). A Taiwanese paper, Commercial Times, reported supply-chain talk that Nvidia may slow chip orders, with AMD and ASIC designers such as Alchip, GUC and MediaTek possibly taking freed capacity. Unconfirmed Nvidia reports on Nov. 17. MediaTek’s October revenue was NT$55.03 billion, up 5.77% from a year earlier and down 0.67% from September.
