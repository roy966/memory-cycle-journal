Chip Stocks Slide Again on AI-Financing Fears, but Korea Says Its HBM Is Already Spoken For

The SOX fell 3.4% on Friday as investors weighed Nvidia’s role as a financier of the AI build-out without Jensen Huang. Sandisk lost 5.6% and Micron 2.5%. In Seoul, analysts and industry officials argue that long-term contracts cover the next two years and that a wider customer base could strengthen memory makers’ hand.

Shares of AI-related companies fell again on Friday, with neoclouds hit hardest, as investors worried about financing after Huang’s exit, Reuters reported. “It’s a short-term death blow to the industry because the amount of vendor financing and financing guarantees that Nvidia and Jensen Huang stepped into are now over,” said Thomas Hayes of Great Hill Capital. Nebius closed down 9.9%, CoreWeave 6.1% and Dell 7.2%; Western Digital fell 5.0%. The FT reported that Nvidia’s board had approached Arm CEO Rene Haas about the job Reported; CNBC said the board has not yet picked a search firm. Over the week, the SOX still rose 8.1% and Micron 12.8%, while Nvidia fell 12.7%.

Korean coverage stressed that existing contracts are intact. SK hynix has said its DRAM and HBM capacity is effectively sold out through 2029, with long-term agreements (LTAs) with 14 major customers; Samsung says its 2027 HBM capacity is fully booked (Korea JoongAng Daily). Daishin Securities estimates SK hynix supplies 55% of Nvidia’s HBM4 this year, Samsung 28% and Micron 17%. “A more diversified customer base could actually strengthen memory suppliers’ bargaining power,” Mirae Asset’s Kim Young-gun wrote. The risk is further out: LTAs could be revised if Nvidia’s schedules change from 2027, and Korean press says SK hynix could face more pricing pressure while Samsung gains from a broader customer mix (via TrendForce).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall: contracts and prices steady, sentiment looser on AI financing. Cycle signal: fundamentals steady, multiples under pressure

HBM · Forecasts

Hana Sees 2027 HBM Demand About 10% Above Supply, With Samsung Taking the Revenue Lead

Hana Securities forecasts 2027 HBM shipments from the three makers at 4.9 billion GB: SK hynix 2.3 billion (47%), Samsung 2.0 billion (41%) and Micron 0.6 billion (12%), Korea Economic TV reported on Nov 13. It expects Samsung to lead on revenue because its HBM4 for Nvidia’s Rubin, built on 1c DRAM with a 4nm base die, carries a higher price, while SK hynix’s large LTA volumes limit its price increases. Monthly HBM wafer capacity rises from 200k to 260k at SK hynix, 190k to 260k at Samsung and 60k to 80k at Micron. Demand from accelerators (Nvidia 8.96 million GPUs, Google 8.80 million TPUs, AMD 0.97 million, Meta and Amazon ASICs above 4 million) adds up to about 5.5 billion GB. An industry official put the balance after the second half of 2028. Hana cut its targets to ₩400,000 for Samsung and ₩2.8 million for SK hynix on sector de-rating (Newspim).

Legal · DRAM

Memory Makers Tell a US Judge the Price Spike Came From AI Demand, Not a Conspiracy

Lawyers for SK hynix, Samsung and Micron argued in San Jose on Friday that a US lawsuit accusing them of restricting supply and fixing DRAM prices describes “lawful oligopoly conduct,” MLex reported; their case is that the AI boom, not a conspiracy, moved the market. Judge Noël Wise took the motion to dismiss under submission; a written ruling will follow. Separately, TrendForce said Nvidia’s supply chain had shown no significant order cuts so far, though it expects AMD and in-house ASIC servers to gain share in 2027.
