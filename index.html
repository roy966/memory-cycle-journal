Kioxia Says NAND Price Gains Are Slowing, Guides Far Below Forecasts

The flash maker sees next-quarter sales up just 1% after a 40% jump, and its finance chief said he is unsure how NAND prices will move next year. Capacity for 2027 is sold out, but memory stocks fell hard on Friday as Intel’s PC warning compounded the worry.

What this shows: Kioxia’s results matched forecasts, but its guide implies almost no growth next quarter. Memory and chip-equipment stocks fell on Friday along with Intel. Kioxia reported after the Tokyo close; Jefferies said the shares were about 9% lower in after-hours trading. Sources: Kioxia via Bloomberg Law, Investing.com and the call transcript; consensus growth from Jefferies; prices from Yahoo Finance; spot prices from TrendForce.

Kioxia’s July–September revenue rose 40% to ¥2.48 trillion, and adjusted operating profit of ¥1.99 trillion (about an 80% margin) was in line. But it guided October–December revenue up just 1%, to about ¥2.5 trillion, when analysts expected about 16%, and operating profit to ¥2.0 trillion against about ¥2.35 trillion (Bloomberg Law, Investing.com). Bits will be flat and prices up only by a low-single-digit percentage, after rises in the mid-20s last quarter and about 70% the quarter before (call transcript). Kioxia blamed the yen, a mix shift to cheaper QLC drives and a large customer’s pricing, and its finance chief said he was “unsure” how NAND prices would move next year (Bloomberg Law, Jefferies).

Demand is not the issue: 2027 capacity is sold out, long-term deals cover more than half of 2028 bits, and Kioxia sees NAND bit demand up in the low 20s in 2027, with the shortage lasting through 2027. It will return all excess cash and declared a first interim dividend of ¥90 (Reuters Japan).

Our call: unchanged overall; volumes are booked into 2028, but NAND price momentum is now clearly fading. Cycle signal: price growth near flat

Markets · Selloff

Memory Stocks Slide as Intel and Kioxia Revive Fears of a Peak

The SOX semiconductor index fell 7.2% on Friday. Intel lost 28.6% after warning that memory costs are shrinking the PC market (MarketWatch), Sandisk fell 13.5% despite raising its guidance (Investing.com), Micron fell 5.3% (Motley Fool), and Western Digital and Seagate fell 7–8% (24/7 Wall St). Samsung and SK hynix are down about 40% and more than 50% from their June peaks, at about three times earnings, despite record profits (Herald Business).

Prices · PC DRAM

PC DRAM Contract Prices Still Rising, but Gains Narrow to 13–18%

TrendForce expects fourth-quarter PC DRAM contract prices to rise 13–18% from the prior quarter, down from 18–23% in the third. Notebook shipments are falling, by an estimated 14% in the third quarter and 9% in the fourth, and PC makers’ DRAM inventory has risen to 7–10 weeks from 6–9. Most talks finish in November, with a small further rise expected (eDaily, RPRNA). DDR5 spot prices stayed firm at $61.50.

Supply · China and equipment

CXMT Earns an 89.5% Gross Margin as Tool Makers Raise Their 2027 Outlook

China’s CXMT said third-quarter revenue rose 613% from a year earlier to 118.7 billion yuan, with net profit of 54.4 billion yuan and a gross margin of 89.53% (MarketScreener). Reports that it will start limited HBM3E output within two months are Unconfirmed (BigGo Finance). Tokyo Electron raised its chip-equipment market forecast to about $160 billion this year and more than $210 billion in 2027, from $150 billion and $190 billion (Investing.com). More tools in 2027 mean more memory supply in 2028, although Jefferies says SK hynix and Samsung are building fab shells first and timing tool move-in to demand.
