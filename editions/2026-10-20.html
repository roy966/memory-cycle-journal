Samsung Has Already Sold 80% of Its 2027 Memory Output, KB Says, but Seoul Marks Chipmakers Down Again

The Kospi fell almost 4% by early afternoon. SK hynix led the losses as regulators said Solidigm’s planned U.S. listing may break Korea’s dual-listing rules. Wall Street’s Monday rebound did not carry over to Seoul.

KB Securities’ head of research, Kim Dong-won, estimates that Samsung has signed long-term agreements covering about 80% of its planned 2027 memory output. That includes five-year contracts for 70% of its conventional DRAM and NAND and commitments for all of its projected HBM. “Competition among hyperscalers to secure supplies continues even for the remaining 20 percent,” he wrote. KB forecasts 2027 operating profit of ₩555 trillion, up 51%, and HBM selling prices up 100–150% as HBM4 rises to 80% of HBM revenue (The Korea Herald). Micron said on Sept. 30 that more than 75% of its 2027 output was already committed.

The market did not trade on it. By 13:20 Seoul time the Kospi was down 3.94% at 5,540.82, after touching 5,493.24 (−4.76%). Foreigners sold ₩1.11 trillion and institutions ₩989 billion, while retail investors bought ₩1.63 trillion. Samsung was down 3.33% and SK hynix 4.62% (Etoday). On Monday the Financial Supervisory Service governor told lawmakers that he and the FSC had judged Solidigm’s possible U.S. IPO to be “a violation of [rules on] dual listing.” The FSC is waiting for a ruling on whether the IPO needs a domestic securities filing (Reuters). A governance group called it an “unprecedented five-tier dual listing” (The Korea Economic Daily).

Shinhan’s Noh Dong-gil says the stocks have fallen on multiples, not earnings. From Sept. 23 to Oct. 19, Samsung fell 23.6% and SK hynix 31.4%, while their 12-month forward EPS estimates rose 1.8% and 3.3%. Forward P/Es are down to 3.4× and 2.9×. “High margins mean high investment costs for customers,” he said, so prices also get renegotiated. Still, “spot memory prices and earnings outlooks remain solid” (Financial News). In New York on Monday the SOX rose 1.61%, ending an eight-day losing streak; Micron gained 2.97% to $904.90 and Sandisk 5.53% (TradingKey).

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027, unchanged; a small nudge looser on near-term commodity pricing (below), offset by more of 2027 under contract. Cycle signal: visibility up, upside capped

Pricing momentum · China and PCs

CXMT’s Profit Growth Slows to 1–4% as DRAM Price Gains Cool, and PC Makers Say Contracts Will End Their Shortage

CXMT guided third-quarter revenue up 15–20% from the second quarter, but net profit up only 1–4%, to RMB 53.4–54.9 billion. Its second-quarter profit had more than doubled. Eastmoney blames moderating DRAM price gains (TrendForce). Chinese module maker Longsys expects third-quarter profit to fall by up to 49% from the second quarter. Over the same three months Taiwan’s Nanya grew revenue 62% (@Schulz_Research on X). CXMT says its fabs are full, and it reports on Oct. 31. In PCs, Lenovo, HP and Dell have signed LTAs with memory makers. One executive said they got “only 70% to 80% of the DRAM and SSDs we requested” in the first half, and “we do not expect major procurement problems next year.” Weaker consumer demand is part of the relief: Omdia says PC shipments fell 21% in the third quarter. DRAM and SSD prices are still expected to rise next year, but more slowly (Electronic Times). Cycle signal: price momentum

Supply · Taiwan

Micron’s Taoyuan Union Calls a Solidarity Strike at Its DRAM and HBM Hub

The union for Micron workers in Taoyuan says it will strike to coincide with the company’s mediation talks with its Taichung union. The dispute is over profit-sharing (Reuters). Micron employs about 15,000 people in Taiwan and makes DRAM and HBM there. Taoyuan mediation failed in September; the Taichung union, at Micron’s largest Taiwan site, has not voted. Reports differ on the date: The Straits Times’ version says Oct. 20, while Commercial Times, also citing Reuters, says the 22nd, the day of the Taichung talks. The union has not said how long the strike will last, and no output impact has been reported. On X, @jukan05 argues that any disruption helps Samsung and SK hynix more than Micron. Date unconfirmed

Verdict
Late upcycle; tight through 2027, unchanged. A small nudge looser near term: CXMT’s profit is up only 1–4% q/q, which Eastmoney puts down to cooling DRAM price gains; Longsys’ profit falls by up to 49%; and PC makers say LTAs and weaker demand end their shortage next year. Visibility is firmer: KB estimates Samsung has ~80% of 2027 booked and all of its HBM committed, and Micron has more than 75%. Spot prices are flat and the 2028 view is unchanged. Flows still drive the stocks: Seoul was down almost 4% despite a Monday rebound in the U.S.
