SemiAnalysis Cuts 2028 HBM Demand by 30% as Memory Stocks Slide Into Nvidia’s Results

The research firm took $1.6 trillion out of its 2027–30 AI accelerator spending forecast, much of it from lower memory prices. Micron fell 7.6% and Sandisk 8.6% on Monday, and Seoul broke below 5,000 on Tuesday. Nvidia reports tonight.

SemiAnalysis revised its AI accelerator model on Nov 13, just 11 days after its last update, and the numbers reached markets on Monday. It now sees 2028 accelerator spending of $758 billion, down 28% from $1.054 trillion, and 2030 spending of $923 billion, down about 40%, according to 36Kr and 456X Research. The cut for 2027 is 19%, to $627 billion, though that still means 60% growth from $392 billion in 2026. Across 2027–30 about $1.6 trillion comes out.

Memory sits at the centre of the change. SemiAnalysis cut its 2028 HBM shipment forecast to 6.4 exabytes from 9.0, a 30% reduction, and 2029 to 7.9 EB from 10.3. One of its stated reasons is that “normalizing HBM supply and demand” will drive down average selling prices. Much of the cut is price rather than volume: by 2030, spending is down about 40% but logic silicon area only 15% and HBM bits 12%. Other reasons include slower revenue growth at frontier labs, tighter data-center financing and a shift toward custom chips. A Jefferies sales note relaying Fubon’s summary says the model pulls the DRAM price peak forward to the third quarter of 2027.

Memory shares took the hit. On Monday the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 5.28% to 9,610.65, 34.3% below its June 22 peak, The Company Chronicle reported. Micron fell 7.62% to $719.02, Sandisk 8.58% to $809.11 and SK hynix’s US depositary receipts 7.09%. On Tuesday the KOSPI fell back below 5,000. At 14:58 Seoul time it was down 2.71% at 4,962.51, with Samsung down 3.25% and SK hynix down 2.23% as foreigners sold a net ₩1.24 trillion, EBN reported. A new US export rule and Nvidia’s leadership uncertainty also weighed (see below).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027. A touch looser again: the 2028 outlook is now in question for HBM pricing and for NAND, but supply through 2027 is still committed. Cycle signal: out-year demand forecasts cut; near-term pricing intact

NAND · DRAM · Forecasts

TrendForce Trims 2027 NAND Demand and Pulls the Contract-Price Decline Forward to the Second Quarter

TrendForce cut its 2027 NAND bit-demand growth to 18.7% from 22.6%, with mobile down 6.5%, client SSD down 10% and enterprise SSD down 1.5% from its prior view, @jukan05 relayed. In a report dated Nov 17, TrendForce says Chinese cloud providers sharply cut 2027 enterprise SSD orders because of GPU shortages, high inventory and weak AI monetization, which fully offsets higher orders from North American providers. With supply still growing fast on new nodes and more QLC, it now expects NAND contract prices to start falling in the second quarter of 2027, a quarter earlier than before, with steeper declines in the second half. DRAM is steadier: TrendForce put 2027 DRAM bit demand at 483 billion GB, 0.3% above its prior view, cutting mobile (−0.6%) and PC (−0.8%) and raising server (+0.8%), the Korea Economic Daily reported.

Policy · Demand

US Closes the Southeast Asia Route for Chinese AI Compute

The Commerce Department issued an interim final rule requiring export licenses for advanced AI chips and servers shipped to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, with an exemption for US-headquartered operators that report. Any data center anywhere now needs a license to provide more than 12 million TPP of computing, roughly 750 H100/H200s, to China-, Macau- or D:5-headquartered customers, including through intermediaries. Existing sites get 90 days. For memory the effect is indirect, through accelerator demand. Nomura, relayed by @pequityresearch, argues pending US China bills would be a net positive for global memory Opinion.

Verdict vs previous call (No. 41, Nov 16)
Late upcycle; tight through 2027. A touch looser again, mostly in the out-years. SemiAnalysis cut 2028 HBM demand by 30% and expects HBM prices to normalize, and TrendForce now sees NAND contract prices falling from the second quarter of 2027. Record Korean capex adds to 2028 supply. Near-term pricing has not changed: DDR5 spot is flat, DRAM contracts are still rising, and brokers say supply is sold out through 1H28. The playbook warning is now on two fronts, NAND (spot far below contract) and 2028 HBM pricing. DRAM has not triggered it yet. Tonight’s Nvidia guide is the next test.
