Memory Stocks Roar Back After Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang

Micron jumped 19% and Seoul’s chip giants more than 6% as Nvidia’s CEO returned after 11 days. Brokers said the selloff had been about lost confidence, not earnings. No new contract-price data came with the rally.

Nvidia’s board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. The company said the board had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident his return “poses no national security risk.” Nvidia rose 31.48%, its best day since 2003, and the chip trade followed. Micron closed at $707.70, up 19.04%, ending a five-day losing streak that had cost it 23.6%. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 14.03% and the Nasdaq 6.51%. Micron is still far below its June record close of $1,213.56. Confirmed

Seoul followed on Tuesday. The KOSPI opened 3.64% higher, set off a buy-side sidecar at about 9:06 a.m., and closed at 5,006.03 (+6.32%). Samsung Electronics ended at ₩189,400 and SK hynix at ₩1,158,000, after intraday highs of ₩196,100 and ₩1,233,000, the Korea Herald reported. Foreign and institutional investors bought and individuals sold. “The recent sell-off was driven by a loss of confidence, not by corporate earnings,” Daishin Securities analyst Lee Kyung-min told the paper. Korean press said the return lowers the risk that Nvidia changes its HBM4 orders or development schedules. KB Securities still sees customers diversifying: it expects Nvidia’s share of global HBM demand to fall from 55–58% this year to 38% in 2027, with Google at 37% and AMD at 13% (Chosun Ilbo). Reported

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027, unchanged. The rally restored confidence but brought no new price data. Forecasts split further: Citi raised its 2027 DRAM call and cut NAND, and Samsung plans a large increase in AI-grade NAND capacity. Cycle signal: DRAM firmer, NAND looser

Sell-side · Contract prices

Citi Lifts Its 2027 DRAM Price Call to 35% and Cuts NAND to 25%

Citi raised its forecast for 2027 DRAM contract prices to a 35% rise from 23%, on HBM prices roughly doubling and resilient server DRAM demand, according to the bank’s Nov. 22 note as reported by Money Today and Unhidea. Its new quarterly path is +5%, +1%, +7% and +11%, against +3%, +2%, +4% and +2% before. It cut 2027 NAND to +25% from +45%, citing weak consumer demand and slowing enterprise-SSD orders in China, with quarters of −3%, −1%, flat and +3%. Citi expects the spot price of a 64GB DDR5 RDIMM, about $3,300 in September and October, to fall to about $2,000 in the first half of 2027 and rebound in the second half, while server DDR5 contracts rise just 1% q/q to $1,823 in the first quarter. On weaker NAND it trimmed SK hynix’s operating-profit estimates by 1.0–3.8% a year and cut its target to ₩2.43 million from ₩3 million. Reported

NAND · Capacity · Samsung

Samsung to Lift AI-Grade NAND Output in Xi’an Fivefold as NAND Prices Slide

Samsung Electronics will raise capacity for its ninth-generation V9 NAND at its second Xi’an fab (X2) from about 10,000 to about 50,000 wafers a month, Seoul Economic Daily reported on Monday, citing industry sources. V9 goes into enterprise SSDs for AI data centers. Samsung will fit out an empty cleanroom, convert 20–30% of its V6 lines, move tools in around February and finish by November 2027. The paper said V9 yields are in the low 90s percent, against the low 80s for V8. Samsung has not confirmed the report. Reported The move adds supply as NAND prices fall. TrendForce expects NAND bit supply to grow 25–30% in 2027 and contract prices to drop 0–5% q/q in the first quarter, as Chinese cloud providers cut SSD orders (Nov. 19).

HBM · Packaging · Orders

Micron Moves First Tool Into Its Singapore HBM Plant as TSMC Reports No Order Cuts

Micron moved its first production tool into its Singapore HBM packaging plant on Nov. 19 and plans initial output in early 2027, Chosun Biz reported, citing industry sources; Singapore’s Economic Development Board also reported the move-in. Micron has guided capex of about $25 billion for the first half of its fiscal 2027. In Taipei, TSMC senior vice president Cliff Hou said no customers had adjusted orders or shipments after recent events (CNA, Newsquawk). In Seoul, KB Securities called Samsung and SK hynix oversold. It said the three DRAM makers’ 2027 HBM is sold out and that much of their DRAM and HBM supply for 2028–29 is already committed (Hankyung). Reported
