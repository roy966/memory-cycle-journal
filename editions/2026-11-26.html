TrendForce Puts a Number on Its Cut: DRAM Gains Could Flatten by Late 2027 as NAND Turns Down

The tracker now sees 2027 DRAM revenue at $1.15 trillion and NAND at $419.3 billion after Chinese cloud providers trimmed orders. Goldman deepened its 2028 price-decline forecasts. Chip stocks clawed back most of an 8% intraday slide.

TrendForce has now put figures on Wednesday’s forecast cut. Chinese cloud providers have reduced their 2027 memory orders, it said, so it lowered its 2027 DRAM revenue forecast to $1.15 trillion (+69% y/y) and its NAND forecast to $419.3 billion (+29%). DRAM stays undersupplied through 2027, but quarterly contract-price increases should “narrow progressively, potentially leveling off in the second half of the year.” North American clouds quickly absorbed the server DRAM that Chinese buyers released outside long-term agreements. NAND is heading into “a downward pricing cycle”: Chinese clouds’ enterprise-SSD cuts since mid-4Q exceed extra North American orders, device makers are trimming storage specs, and supply will grow significantly. @dnystedt notes the old figures were not widely published and both forecasts are still above TrendForce’s May release. Confirmed

The banks are moving the same way. Goldman Sachs now expects mainstream DRAM prices to fall 19% and NAND 21% in 2028, versus −5% and −14% before, while seeing a severe shortage through 2027, Maeil Business reported. Sandisk and Kioxia are down 50% and 45% in a month, against roughly 20% for Samsung and SK hynix. Reported On the other side, HSBC expects enterprise-SSD demand to grow at a 65% annual rate over 2025–28 (via @pequityresearch).

The tape stabilized. The SOX fell as much as 8% on Wednesday before closing down 0.65% at 8,774.86; Micron ended at $601.25 (−4.53%), then $615.21 after hours. Nvidia fell 3.91% to $149.30 after S&P Global Ratings put its AA rating on CreditWatch negative; hot PCE inflation data and new US tariff proposals weighed early (Motley Fool, Aju). In Seoul, the Bank of Korea raised rates to 3.25% and lifted its 2027 growth forecast to 4.1% on chip exports. The KOSPI gave back most of a 2.6% early gain to close at 4,710.65 (+0.28%), with SK hynix at ₩1,053,000 (+2.23%) and Samsung at ₩171,300 (+0.35%). US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 in servers and HBM, a touch looser. The size of the TrendForce cut matters more than the cut itself: DRAM price gains could stop in 2H27, a quarter or two before the 2028 debate starts. Cycle signal: DRAM gains fading, NAND down

Micron · Taiwan · Labor

Micron’s Taoyuan Talks Resume Under a Reported Threat to $48.5 Billion of Investment

Micron and its Taoyuan union were due to meet again on Thursday afternoon, Taiwan time, after 84.6% of voting members rejected the company’s offer. Before the talks, Wealth Magazine chairman Hsieh Chin-ho said senior Micron executives had told him the dispute could lead the board to reconsider NT$1.6 trillion (about $48.5 billion) of follow-on Taiwan investment, shift it to Singapore and, in a worst case, close the Taoyuan fab with about 3,000 job cuts (Commercial Times via Investing.com). Unconfirmed The union accused Micron of applying public pressure before every round and rejects a peace clause barring strikes for up to 10 years (United Daily News, Commercial Times). Taiwan makes about 60% of Micron’s DRAM. No outcome had been reported by build time. A disruption would support DRAM prices but cut Micron’s volume.

Policy · Korea · Contracts

Washington Warns Korea’s Memory Build-Out Could Cause a Glut

The US Trade Representative’s Section 301 findings on Korea say that its “continued expansion of its domestic memory chip production to capitalize on AI demand contributes to a potential overcapacity crisis… Even if demand remains high, oversupply could arrive as soon as the new capacity comes online.” USTR proposed tariffs within the 15% ceiling, with an option of up to 25% (Korea JoongAng Daily). Memory chips themselves are excluded from the proposed tariffs (DigiTimes). Confirmed Separately, Yonhap Infomax reported that the long-term agreements both companies call binding are hard to see in their filings. SK hynix’s contract liabilities have stayed at about ₩500 billion for a year; it says it uses deposits, escrow and guarantees rather than prepayments. Samsung’s grew only about ₩550 billion in the third quarter, after ₩8.5 trillion in the second, even though it has signed more contracts. Lower prepayments give customers more room to walk away if the market turns, the report said. Reported

NAND · Technology · China

Samsung and SK Hynix License YMTC’s Bonding Technology as Samsung Readies 500-Layer NAND

Samsung and SK hynix have struck cross-licensing deals for elements of YMTC’s Xtacking wafer-bonding technology, Nikkei reported, because hybrid bonding becomes necessary at 400-plus layers and YMTC’s patents are hard to avoid. Samsung had already licensed YMTC’s hybrid-bonding patents in 2025. Reported Samsung has developed a NAND with more than 500 layers, offering 50% more capacity per chip than its 400-layer part, and will present it at ISSCC in February, aiming for launch as early as next year, Seoul Economic Daily reported. Pilot yields are about 30%.
