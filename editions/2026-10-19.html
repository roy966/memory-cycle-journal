CXMT Could Add 31% More DRAM Capacity in 2027 and Nearly Double by Late 2028, Putting a Date on the Glut Debate

Gartner sees DRAM prices falling from late 2027; Micron expects 2028 to be tighter still. CXMT’s own shares fell 6.5% on Monday despite a ¥131 billion nine-month profit.

Sources: Counterpoint & TrendForce via Korean media; CNBC; Seoul Economic Daily; Kiwoom via Yeouido News (Friday data); The Korea Herald; TrendForce.

Korean media, citing Counterpoint and TrendForce estimates, put CXMT at about 320,000 wafers a month now, 420,000 in 2027 (+31%) and 500,000–600,000 by late 2028 (SquawkNews summary of The Korea Herald and eToday). SemiAnalysis has a lower path: 350,000 at end-2026 to 500,000 at end-2028 (JoongAng via Korea Daily). Counterpoint has CXMT at 10% of global DRAM revenue in the third quarter, up from 5% a year earlier (CNBC).

The incumbents are adding too: Samsung is weighing pulling P5 tool move-in forward to 2Q27, and SK hynix opens its first Yongin cleanroom in February 2027 (Korea Daily). The forecasts split on what follows: Gartner sees DRAM prices falling from late 2027 or the first half of 2028, sharply in 2028, while Micron expects the 2028 shortage to be worse than this year’s. Jefferies’ European team says CXMT’s 4F² cell gives about 30% more bits at the same node without new lithography; ASM estimates it needs an extra $400–450 million of deposition tools per 100,000 wafers a month.

CXMT’s stock fell 6.5% on Monday after guiding nine-month net profit to ¥131–132.5 billion, against a ¥5.28 billion loss a year earlier (CNBC).

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027, unchanged; a small nudge looser for 2028, a touch firmer near-term (below). Cycle signal: 2028 supply

Korea · Monday session

Seoul Opens 3% Lower on DeepSeek and AI-Funding Fears, Then Steadies as Institutions Buy; Hana Says Q4 DRAM Prices Will Beat Forecasts

The Kospi opened at 5,488.41, down 3.17% (ChosunBiz). Samsung and SK hynix each fell more than 4% before paring losses (Bloomberg). The trigger was DeepSeek’s weekend V4.1-Flash update, which it calls faster and leaner, reviving worries that AI needs less memory. By about 13:40 Seoul time the Kospi was up 0.45% (Yahoo Finance), as institutions bought. Last week was both chipmakers’ worst since 2008 (The Korea Herald). Forced selling of margin-loan positions rose 12.7-fold from Oct 8 to Oct 15 (Seoul Economic Daily). Hana Securities says fourth-quarter DRAM price rises will exceed earlier forecasts and the selloff is excessive. But with memory operating margins above 80%, “it will be difficult to continue aggressive price increases” (Yonhap Infomax). At the low end, some Chinese phone brands are reportedly buying salvaged memory and cutting output (TrendForce, citing TechNews). Unconfirmed

HBM · Nvidia’s custom memory

Nvidia Puts All Three HBM Makers on TSMC 3nm for Its Custom HBM, and Samsung Breaks Its Turnkey Model

Nvidia asked Samsung, SK hynix and Micron to make the base die of NVHBM, its custom HBM built on the HBM4E design, on TSMC’s 3nm process, with core dies standardized on 1c DRAM (Seoul Economic Daily; Reuters followed). Samsung accepted. It is the first time Samsung has worked with TSMC at the HBM manufacturing stage. It keeps its own 4nm base die for standard HBM4/HBM4E and for customers such as AMD, and is checking whether its own foundry could take NVHBM volume “in case NVHBM demand exceeds TSMC’s production capacity.” One design lets Nvidia qualify once and shift volume between suppliers. Cycle signal: buyer leverage
