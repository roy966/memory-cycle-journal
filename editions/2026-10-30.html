Sandisk Beats and Guides Higher, but Growth Now Comes More From Volume Than Price

Data-center revenue jumped 61% in a quarter and half of next year’s bits are already under long-term deals. Price rises were smaller than investors hoped, the shares fell after hours, and Korean and Japanese memory stocks sold off on Friday.

What this shows: Sandisk beat on every line, but memory shares fell anyway. Micron and Sandisk dropped Thursday as Alphabet’s spending scare spread, and Korea and Japan followed on Friday after Intel and Amazon fell after hours. Estimates are those cited by Wall St Engine on X. Korean and Japanese prices are Friday closes (15:30 local, 08:30 IST). Sources: Sandisk, Intel, Reuters, CNBC, Yahoo Finance.

Sandisk’s fiscal first-quarter revenue was $11.03 billion, up 23% from the prior quarter and above the $10.64 billion estimate. Adjusted gross margin was 85.1% and adjusted EPS $49.51 against $46.22 expected (company release; estimates cited by Wall St Engine). Data-center revenue rose 61% to $4.80 billion, Edge (PCs and phones) rose 7% to $5.83 billion and Consumer fell 29% to $397 million. Sandisk guided the December quarter to $12.2–12.6 billion of revenue, an 84.5–85.5% margin and $55–57 of EPS before an 8-for-1 split. The split takes effect after the close on Nov. 19, and the shares trade on a split-adjusted basis from Nov. 20. It also bought back $5.0 billion of stock in the quarter.

The catch was the mix. On the call, management said about 60% of revenue growth came from volume and 40% from price. It expects “modest” price increases through the year (Benzinga transcript). Sandisk says about half of fiscal 2027 bits and about two-thirds of fiscal 2028 bits are under long-term deals. Its deal pipeline exceeds supply “through the end of the decade”, and 95% of those deals are with investment-grade customers. It sees data-center NAND bit demand growing about 40% in 2027, to more than 55% of the market. Jefferies (Buy) said slower pricing and softer Edge and Consumer sales “elevate concerns that supply is starting to catch up”. Goldman (Buy) pointed to “very limited supply additions”. The stock had already fallen 5.3% on Thursday and lost more after hours.

Our call: unchanged overall; enterprise NAND and AI demand still tight, but price momentum is slowing and PC demand is cracking. Cycle signal: price growth slows

Demand · PCs

Intel Says Memory Prices Are Shrinking the PC Market, and Guides Below Forecasts

Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion and adjusted EPS to $0.25, both below estimates, and the shares fell about 20% after hours (Reuters, CNBC). It expects PC-chip revenue to fall by a mid-20s percentage. It sees the PC market shrinking about 12% in units in 2026 and about 10% in 2027, as higher memory prices and shortages raise system costs. Cantor’s math implies PC units down 45% from a year earlier in the fourth quarter. BluBird also notes a roughly two-week shutdown at Intel’s Kiryat Gat fab. This is the demand destruction the cycle tops have shown before: high prices start to cut volumes at the consumer end. Demand · PC units falling

Demand · Hyperscalers

Amazon Says 2027 Spending Will Be “Meaningfully” Higher as AWS Growth Hits 39%

AWS revenue rose 39.3% to $46.0 billion, its fastest growth in nearly five years. Amazon kept 2026 capex at about $220 billion and said 2027 will be “meaningfully more” (company release, CNBC). The shares had already fallen 4.3% on Thursday and dropped further after hours as spending outran even the faster cloud growth. A bigger 2027 budget supports AI memory demand, even as investors sell the spenders. AI demand · intact

Supply · NAND

SK Hynix Says No Decision on a U.S. NAND Fab After Report of a ₩60 Trillion Plan

Korea’s Maeil Business Newspaper reported that Solidigm, SK hynix’s U.S. unit, is pursuing about ₩60 trillion for a new NAND fab in the United States. In an SEC filing, SK hynix said Solidigm is “reviewing various measures” to expand capacity but “no matters have been determined”. It will update within a month. Some headlines overnight said SK hynix “will construct” the plant; the company has not confirmed that. Separately, OpenAI’s Sam Altman said memory makers could each make about $100 billion a year of free cash flow but hesitate to build fabs, and that “the invisible hand is failing” (TNW). Both point the same way: buyers are pushing for more supply. Unconfirmed plan 2028+ supply risk
