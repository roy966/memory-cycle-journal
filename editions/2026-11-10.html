Memory Stocks Snap Back as TrendForce Sees a 2027 Server-DRAM Gap

The SOX gained 6.85% on Monday and Micron 6.25%; in Seoul SK hynix closed +6.69% as foreigners bought for the first time in eight sessions. TrendForce says 2027 server DRAM supply grows about 25%, slower than server CPU demand, though DRAM lead times eased a week to 18. Spot edged lower again.

What this shows: the equity rebound was broad (SK hynix ADR +5.53%, Applied Materials +11.37%), but Nvidia fell on its CEO probe. DRAM spot did not join the rally: DDR5 flat, DDR4 down a fourth session. NAND wafer spot ($16.15, −4.33% w/w) is carried from the last weekly print. Monday’s KOSPI final (5,018.85) revises No. 34’s preliminary 5,019.78. Sources: KRX via Naver Finance, Yonhap, Yahoo Finance, FnNews, TSMC, TrendForce.

The KOSPI opened 4.55% higher at 5,247.22 and touched 5,300.92 (+5.62%) before easing; buy-side sidecars were triggered on the KOSPI at 09:06 and the KOSDAQ at 09:10 KST. By 10:32 KST foreigners were net buyers for the first time in eight sessions, after selling an average ₩1.79 trillion a day over the previous seven (Yonhap); preliminary close data on Naver show a ₩353.2bn foreign net buy. Daishin’s Lee Kyung-min said data-center permit delays “may slow the pace of investment execution, but will not directly lead to a decrease in the total volume” of AI spending (MoneyToday). On Wall Street, AMD rose 13.26% and Broadcom 8.78%; Nvidia closed at $178.67 after a low of $170.24.

The fundamentals news came from TrendForce. Analyst Hsu Chia-yuan expects 2027 server CPU shipments to rise at least 30% (base case 40–45%), against server DRAM bit supply growth of about 25%; even counting SOCAMM, supply grows only 35–40%. Total DRAM bit supply growth slows from 31% in 2026 to 24% in 2027 as node-migration gains fade, then recovers above 25% in 2028 as new fabs open. Server DRAM plus HBM reaches 65% of DRAM supply by end-2027, squeezing other applications (China Times). Its weekly survey put DRAM lead times at 18 weeks (from 19), eSSD at 16 (balance 8) and ABF substrates at 48–56 (balance 12). US hyperscalers’ server-DRAM demand is firm, while Chinese CSPs are trimming high-capacity RDIMM purchases (Digital Times).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall. Supply evidence is a touch tighter (TrendForce’s 2027 server-DRAM gap, EUV scarcity), while price momentum is a touch softer (DDR4 spot down again, lead times one week shorter). Cycle signal: stocks rebound; spot still soft

Capex · Lithography

Samsung and SK Hynix Race for ASML EUV Tools

Samsung and SK hynix are each committing ₩10–20 trillion to ASML EUV scanners and may order earlier than planned because tool lead times have reportedly doubled, according to Solution News, cited by TrendForce. SK hynix has disclosed an EUV purchase of about ₩11.95 trillion, for delivery by Dec. 31, 2027, to move to 1c DRAM. Samsung is adding 1c lines for HBM4 at Pyeongtaek P4. It has ordered lithography tools for P5, whose first fab is targeted for 2028; equipment moves in after the first-phase cleanroom is finished in the first half of 2027. Both plan 1d DRAM in 2027 and limited sub-10nm “0a” output in 2028, with High-NA EUV on one or two layers. ASML said in October that its supply will fall short of demand in 2027 and 2028; memory was 46% of its Q3 system sales (TrendForce, Nov 10).

Taiwan · Monthly data

TSMC and Taiwan Exports Set Records; Nanya’s Monthly Gain Slows to 3%

TSMC’s October revenue was NT$586.89 billion, up 14.7% from September and 59.7% from a year earlier (TSMC). Taiwan’s October exports hit a record US$96.76 billion, up 58.9%, with integrated circuits up 63.0% to $31.17 billion (Ministry of Finance). Nanya Technology’s October revenue was a 12th straight monthly record at NT$46.48 billion, up 487.7% year on year but only 3.07% from September (Anue). MediaTek’s October sales were not out at press time.
