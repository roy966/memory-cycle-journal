Jassy Says the Memory Shortage Won’t End in 2027 as AWS Sells Out

Amazon’s cloud chief says memory is the constraint, heading into the cycle’s biggest earnings week.

“There’s only so much memory, and I don’t think that that shortage ends in 2027,” Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said on an a16z podcast released Friday and widely recirculated over the weekend. AWS capacity for 2026 is sold out, 2027 is “largely sold out” and “a good chunk of ’28 is sold out,” he said. AWS has to choose between HBM for accelerators and DRAM for its core cloud, and 2026 capex of about $220 billion could go higher (Investing.com, Mint). Amazon reports Q3 on Thursday.

Pricing data point the same way. Barclays, cited by Korea’s Digital Daily on Sunday, says DDR4 contract prices rose 24% over three months against 20% for DDR5, so older parts are rising faster than new ones. A global financial firm in the same report sees the supply-demand gap peaking at 13.9% in 4Q27, with the shortage possibly lasting to 2029. Nomura’s memory team, via X, says “2027 memory is basically already booked. 2028 is the bigger question.”

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027, unchanged; a touch firmer on duration (Jassy, Nomura, LTAs reaching mobile), with positioning the main risk into the SK hynix, Samsung and Sandisk prints. Cycle signal: demand visibility extends

Korea · Earnings week

SK Hynix Reports Wednesday, With the Street Looking for 78% Margins and More Buybacks

FnGuide consensus for SK hynix’s third quarter is ₩98.23 trillion of revenue and ₩76.96 trillion of operating profit, about a 78% margin. The profit estimate is down 5.6% from three months ago, mostly on the won (FnNews). Yonhap puts Samsung and SK hynix at about ₩185 trillion of combined Q3 operating profit, with HBM4 revenue for Nvidia’s Vera Rubin now in the numbers and long-term agreements spreading to mobile. The Street expects another shareholder return of at least ₩30 trillion on top of August’s ₩40 trillion (Newsis). Korean retail has leaned in: about $300 million of net buying in 14 single-stock leveraged products on Samsung and SK hynix and $3.55 billion in SOXL from Oct. 1–23 (Chosun Ilbo). China · DRAM supply

Taiwan Report Says CXMT Is Not Undercutting Rivals on DDR5

Unconfirmed A vice president at a Taiwanese server maker says CXMT declined to sell it DDR5, and a veteran analyst says Apple asked CXMT for prices and found them as high as the big three’s (TechNews/Business Weekly, Oct. 25). About 90% of CXMT’s output goes to Chinese brands. Isaiah Research sees CXMT at 300,000 wafers a month by end-2026 and 360,000 in 2027 with its Shanghai fab. Nanya, with an 85.7% Q3 gross margin, would meet CXMT head-on in DDR5 around end-2027. Single-source color; if it holds, China is adding capacity without dumping for now. CXMT’s Q3 results are due by Oct. 31. Supply · unconfirmed

Storage · Micron labor

Seagate and Sandisk Report This Week as Micron’s Taiwan Union Returns to Talks

Seagate reports Tuesday after the close (call 23:00 IST): guidance is $4.0–4.2 billion of revenue and EPS of $7.10–7.50, against consensus of about $4.14 billion and $7.40. Sandisk follows Thursday with guidance of $10.3–10.8 billion, 83–85% gross margin and EPS of $44–46; consensus EPS of about $46.20 is above the top of the range (TradingView). Cantor cut its Sandisk target to $2,200 from $2,900 on multiple, not earnings. Micron’s Taoyuan union meets management Tuesday after members rejected the offer 1,633 to 1,637. Its Oct. 7 strike mandate stays active and no date will be pre-announced (UDN, Focus Taiwan). NAND test · labor risk
