Memory Stocks Slide Again as a Korean Broker Warns NAND Will Turn First

Seoul gave back Monday’s rebound as the US chip selloff spread. Korea Investment & Securities cut its Samsung target, and BNK said flash-memory prices are already rolling over while DRAM holds up.

Korean stocks are at 14:15 KST (07:15 IST). Sources: Financial News, Seoul Economic Daily, Hankyung, Yonhap, Gotrade, Investing.com, TrendForce.

The KOSPI fell 3.62% to 5,647.22 on Tuesday, erasing Monday’s 2.24% gain, and the KOSDAQ lost 4.08%. Samsung was down 3.06% at ₩222,000 and SK hynix 3.74% at ₩1,417,000 at 2:15 p.m. In New York on Monday, Micron fell 5.78% to $833.59, Sandisk 6.59% to $1,075.08 and the Philadelphia semiconductor index 3.37%, as Kioxia’s flat December-quarter guide and worries about AI spending weighed (Gotrade, Investing.com).

Korea Investment & Securities cut its Samsung target to ₩440,000 from ₩570,000 and kept a Buy rating. It forecasts fourth-quarter revenue of ₩216.4 trillion and operating profit of ₩123.3 trillion, with DRAM and NAND prices up 7% and 6% from the third quarter. It warned that price gains “could be limited” from next year (Seoul Economic Daily). BNK Investment’s Lee Min-hee went further on flash: NAND leads DRAM in the cycle, Kioxia’s flat guide suggests NAND price rises are stopping, NAND spot prices have been falling since April, and WSTS data show the global NAND average price fell 1.6% in September from August, the first drop in a year (Hankyung).

Our call: unchanged overall; NAND looser again, DRAM steady. Cycle signal: NAND second derivative turning, DRAM rate of increase slowing but prices still rising

Demand · Customers

Apple Says Memory Costs Will Keep Rising, and It Is Committing More Cash to Secure Supply

Apple guided December-quarter gross margin to 45–46%, down from 47.9% in the September quarter, as memory costs rise further. Chief financial officer Kevan Parekh said memory costs will keep rising into next year. Asked about a long-term supply deal, operations chief Sabih Khan did not confirm one but said Apple is working closely with memory suppliers and its purchase commitments rose $33 billion in the quarter (Reuters). Chief executive John Ternus called memory pricing “unprecedented” (MacRumors).

Fact check · LTAs

Samsung’s Long-Term Contract Goal Is 60–70% of Capacity by 2030, Not 70–80%

TrendForce’s Weekly Radar 008 (Nov 2) says Samsung “targets 70–80% of capacity under LTAs and SK hynix 50%,” without citing a source. That does not match Samsung’s own earnings call on Oct 29: it has signed long-term agreements with 10 customers, is negotiating with 10 more, and aims to cover 60–70% of total capacity by 2030 (ChosunBiz, Korea JoongAng Daily). The higher figure probably mixes in other estimates. KB Securities said about 75–80% of 2027 planned output is already booked (Korea Herald), and a September TrendForce report put about 70% under contract through 2031. We keep 60–70% by 2030 as Samsung’s stated target.

HBM · Customers · China

Altman Courts Korea’s Memory Makers as a Report Details an HBM Route to China

OpenAI’s Sam Altman met about 50 Samsung affiliate presidents in Yongin on Oct 31 and lunched with SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won; HBM supply was expected to be on the agenda, but no deal was announced (CNBC, Herald Business). Separately, The Information reports that smugglers ship high-bandwidth memory to China bundled with lower-performance server processors, which exploits export rules that rate a package by its processor rather than its HBM content Report · paywalled.
