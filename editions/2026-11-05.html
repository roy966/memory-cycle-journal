Blue-Wave Sell-Off Hits Memory Stocks as Suppliers Say the Shortage Gets Worse

Seoul fell about 5.5% as investors priced slower US data-center building. On the same day, a module maker and a Korean broker both said 2027 supply is already spoken for.

The US midterm results kept hitting AI hardware. On Wednesday the Dow rose 3.03% and the S&P 500 1.58%, but semiconductors fell. The SOX lost 3.13% to a six-month low, Micron 7.00%, Sandisk 7.91% and Nvidia 3.17%. (Yahoo Finance, Investing.com, Chosun Ilbo). In Seoul on Thursday, the KOSPI opened 1.82% lower and a sell sidecar was triggered at 10:16. Another followed on the KOSDAQ at 14:27. By 14:38 the KOSPI was down 5.39%, SK hynix 9.31% and Samsung 7.68%; Samsung Electro-Mechanics was down nearly 15% (Yonhap, NewDaily). In Tokyo, Kioxia fell for a fourth straight day, back below ¥10,000 (Kabutan).

Supply news pointed the other way. Team Group’s chief executive, Chen Ching-wen, said the worst of the shortage runs from the fourth quarter through the end of the second quarter of 2027, with “no downward trend in price whatsoever”, and that the market may not balance until the second half of 2028 or later (Tom’s Hardware). Meritz Securities says customer orders for 2027–28 far exceed supply, and that a slower US build-out would not change that much (via X). Spot prices are slipping, though: DDR5 16Gb fell 1.09% and 512Gb TLC NAND wafers 4.33% in a week (TrendForce).

Our call: unchanged overall; DRAM steady as spot edges lower, NAND a touch looser; policy risk rising. Cycle signal: supply tight, stocks pricing slower demand

Demand · Phones · Data center

Qualcomm Says Memory Costs Are Shrinking the Phone Business

Qualcomm’s fiscal fourth-quarter revenue was $10.44 billion and adjusted EPS $2.17. Handset revenue fell 26% to $5.14 billion “as rising memory costs force handset makers to raise prices”. Qualcomm expects the handset market to be flat in 2027, and guided next-quarter EPS to $2.15–2.35, against about $2.27 expected. The shares fell about 7% after hours. Data-center revenue was $650 million, toward a $5 billion target for fiscal 2027; chief executive Cristiano Amon said, “We don’t have a demand issue; it’s a supply issue” (CNBC). Like AMD in PCs, this is high memory prices cutting consumer device demand, which the playbook flags as a late-cycle signal.

Supply · Taiwan · Modules

A Module Maker Warns That “Even Money Can’t Buy Goods”

On its Nov 2 earnings call, Team Group said spot prices are 30–50% above contract and that the DDR5 16Gb contract price rose from $8.70 in October 2025 to $48 in September 2026. It said Chinese makers CXMT and YMTC are nearing a 10% share, and that business customers now make up 64.6% of its sales. The shortage could reach a point where “even money can’t buy goods”, Chen said, and will “get worse next year” (Tom’s Hardware, BigGo Finance). Module makers buy at spot and contract, so this is a buyer’s view of supply.

HBM · Foundry · Materials

Samsung Plans a Giheung Fab for HBM Base Dies; a Taiwan Fire Hits AI-Server Laminate

Samsung plans an advanced foundry fab, “Project Kairos”, at its Giheung campus, at an estimated ₩25 trillion. It would make 2nm base dies for HBM5 and 4nm dies for HBM4/4E, with the building finished in late 2028 (Digitimes, Financial News, Seoul Economic Daily) Report. Samsung’s second Taylor fab is reported fully booked for HBM base dies (TrendForce, via X). Early on Nov 4, a fire hit Panasonic’s Hukou plant in Hsinchu, which makes MEGTRON laminate for AI-server boards: about 20% of MEGTRON capacity and about 4% of global high-end laminate. Output can move to Japan and China, and the industry sees limited immediate impact (Commercial Times, Economic Daily News).

Verdict vs previous call (No. 29, Nov 4)
Late upcycle; tight through 2027 and booked into 2028. Unchanged overall. Supply signals firmed again: Team Group sees the worst shortage through mid-2027 and balance in 2H28 or later, Meritz says 2027–28 orders exceed supply, and JPMorgan and Nomura see HBM and DRAM prices staying high. But the edges loosened a little. DDR5 spot fell for the first time in a week, NAND wafer prices dropped 4.33%, and Qualcomm blamed memory costs for a 26% fall in handset revenue. Playbook: softer spot under rising contract, and consumer units hurt by prices, are both late-cycle signs. The stock sell-off (Micron −7%, SK hynix about −9%) is about US data-center timing, not memory pricing. Next tests: payrolls Friday, TSMC October sales on Nov 10, and Nvidia on Nov 17.
