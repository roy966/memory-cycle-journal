The midterm sell-off got worse. On Thursday the PHLX Semiconductor Index lost 12.25% to 9,011.14, its worst day since March 16, 2020. Micron closed at $656.39 (−17.46%), Sandisk at $806.43 (−22.26%, worst in the S&P 500), and the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52%. Nvidia dropped about 9% (MarketWatch via Morningstar; Exa). Analysts framed it as uncertainty over AI data-center permitting and regulation after the Democratic midterm gains — Yorkville’s Daniel Ives said the build-out looks “slower and a longer process than it looked like 48 hours ago.”

Seoul followed. Thursday’s final close corrected our provisional prints: KOSPI 5,383.80 (−5.28%), Samsung ₩201,500 (−7.57%), SK hynix ₩1,264,000 (−9.46%), with foreigners net selling ₩3.39 trillion (Korea Herald). On Friday the KOSPI opened at 5,263.63 (−2.2%) and hit a Level-1 circuit breaker within about three minutes as the index fell roughly 8.5% into the 4,900s — the first sub-5,000 print since Feb 2. Premarket depths had Samsung as much as −13% and SK hynix −15.3%; at 9:14 a.m. they were −9.98% and −11.71%. By the afternoon the index had reclaimed 5,000 on institutional and pension buying before finishing near 5,042.25 (−6.34%) on Yahoo’s near-close print, with foreigners still selling (Korea Herald, Sedaily, Yahoo).

Supply news still pointed the other way. Counterpoint put Q3 DRAM revenue near $200 billion on only about 4% bit growth. TechInsights’ Jeongdong Choe still puts supply at about 85% of demand. TrendForce sees overall DRAM bit supply growth slowing to 24% in 2027 and remaining tight. Spot DRAM, though, fell for a second session: DDR5 16Gb to $60.167 (−0.82%), DDR4 16Gb to $80.938 (−0.91%). The 512Gb TLC wafer weekly print is still $16.152 (−4.33% w/w from Nov 4).

Our call: unchanged overall on supply; policy and sentiment risk up sharply; DRAM spot a second down day, still elevated. Cycle signal: stocks pricing slower demand; fundamentals still tight

Supply · Taiwan · Labor

Micron Pauses a $3–4.5B Taiwan Fab Over a Labor Fight

Bloomberg, citing people familiar, says Micron has all but halted construction of Fab 11E in Taoyuan — a $3–4.5 billion HBM and DRAM project including ASML tools, due around 2028. Only minor safety work remains. Executives worry a Taichung union deal could be overturned as the smaller Taoyuan union (seeking 15% of operating profit, all cash) expands. Micron did not address the pause: Taiwan ops “continue as normal” and “we look forward to reaching an agreement.” Taiwan is about 60% of Micron’s conventional DRAM output. A delayed 2028 shell is a supply-side wrinkle in an already-short market (Taipei Times / Bloomberg, Focus Taiwan) Report.

Demand · Pricing · Share

Counterpoint: Q3 DRAM Near $200B on Just 4% Bit Growth

“This is a pricing super-cycle, not yet a volume cycle,” Counterpoint’s Jeongku Choi wrote. Bit shipments rose only about 4%, yet DRAM revenue reached nearly $200 billion — about 5× a year ago, or +369% y/y — with ASPs at about $1.9 per Gb. Shares: Samsung 38%, SK hynix 29% (revenue up more than 40% q/q), Micron 21%, CXMT 9%. MS Hwang said HBM prices are catching commodity DRAM, LTAs support prices, and “major new fabs will not start production until 2028.” Conventional DDR/LPDDR revenue rose about 500%/nearly 400% y/y as agentic AI lifts CPU DRAM (Vera Rubin ~1.5 TB per socket). Samsung is expected to take the HBM lead next year on HBM4 (Counterpoint, Nov 5).
