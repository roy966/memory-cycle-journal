Jensen Huang Quits Nvidia, and the Memory Question Becomes Who Gets the HBM

Nvidia’s board accepted Huang’s resignation; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Nvidia fell 4.3% and Micron 1.6%. Samsung told BofA it has seen no order cuts, and analysts say Nvidia’s discounted HBM terms may not outlast him. Seoul opened up 2–3% and then turned lower.

What this shows: the market priced a leadership shock, not a demand shock. Nvidia recovered from −13.1% at the open; AMD (+5.86%) and Broadcom (+5.54%) rose. Memory stocks slipped. DDR5 spot was flat for a third session; DDR4 kept easing. NAND wafer spot ($16.15) is still the Nov 2 print. Sources: Nvidia, Reuters via Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, KRX via Naver Finance, Herald Business, TrendForce.

Nvidia said its board had accepted Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. Kress stays CFO while the board searches inside and outside the company, and director Harvey Jones becomes Nvidia’s first board chair. The company said customer commitments and the product roadmap continue without interruption; it reports fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17 as scheduled. Reuters, citing supply-chain sources, said the chips Nvidia ships over the next year or two are already designed and in production. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving, and the Justice Department is reported to be examining Nvidia on antitrust grounds Reported.

For memory, the open question is pricing power. SemiAnalysis estimates SK hynix’s 2027 HBM4 price at roughly $3.0–3.3 per gigabit for Nvidia versus $3.7–4.1 for AMD, Broadcom, Amazon and Google, and expects Nvidia’s preferential access to weaken without Huang. “If another company fills the gap left by Nvidia, it will still need our HBM,” the Korea Semiconductor Industry Association’s Ahn Ki-hyun told The Korea Herald. Samsung told BofA it has seen no order cuts tied to a slowdown in US data-center building, and Cerebras’s CEO expects memory shortages and price increases to run through 2028 (both via X). In Seoul, the KOSPI opened 2.05% higher and briefly topped 5,500 before foreign investors turned net sellers; it closed at 5,290.54 (−0.98%). Samsung closed at ₩206,000 (−0.96%) and SK hynix at ₩1,216,000 (−3.03%).

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall: supply a touch tighter, demand quality still the risk. Cycle signal: supply tighter, pricing power shifting to memory

Equipment · Supply

Applied Materials Beats as China’s SMIC Says Memory Won’t Balance Until Mid-to-Late 2028

Applied Materials’ fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 53% to $10.41 billion, ahead of the $10.30 billion consensus, and it guided the January quarter to about $11.10 billion against $10.89 billion expected (Reuters). Shares rose about 3% after hours. SMIC posted record third-quarter revenue of $3.19 billion (+33.9%) and 97.3% utilization. Co-CEO Zhao Haijun said capacity stays severely constrained through 2027 and shortages may extend into mid-to-late 2028; AI-related memory supply and demand could reach a rough balance after mid-2028. He said suppliers now quote 16–24 months for new equipment orders (TrendForce, citing IT Home and Cailian Press). TrendForce also reported that TSMC, which denied plans to make 3D DRAM, is involved in HBM base dies and wafer-to-wafer stacking.

Policy · China · Deals

Senator Asks Commerce to Blacklist CXMT as Samsung Weighs a Data-Center Cooling Deal

Sen. Tom Cotton asked Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to add CXMT to the Entity List, cutting it off from US tools and technical support for all its fabs, and to explore banning its products from the US market (CNBC, Reuters). Counterpoint data cited by CNBC put CXMT at 10% of DRAM revenue in 2Q26, up from 4% a year earlier. Cotton’s letter says CXMT’s HBM3E yields hover around 20%. Commerce has not said whether it will act; separately, Reuters reported the White House is discussing Lutnick’s future Reported. Samsung is in talks to buy US data-center cooling maker Modine, valued at about $5.6 billion, Bloomberg reported; Modine rose about 29% after hours and neither company commented Unconfirmed.
