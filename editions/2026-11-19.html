Micron Says Memory Will Be Even Tighter in 2027 and 2028, With 26 Long-Term Deals Behind It

Three-quarters of next year’s output is sold and 2028 volumes are under discussion, under agreements carrying $32 billion upfront and take-or-pay terms. Samsung called the build-out structural. The shares did not follow.

Micron chief executive Sanjay Mehrotra and chief technology officer Scott DeBoer told the TomorrowX Summit in Austin on Wednesday that 75% of the company’s 2027 production is already sold and that it is talking to customers about 2028 commitments. Calendar 2027 and 2028 “will be even tighter” than 2026, the company said, Seeking Alpha reported. Micron has signed 26 supply and collaboration agreements carrying $32 billion in upfront cash and financial commitments. Many run five years or longer and include take-or-pay provisions, cash deposits and pricing bands, according to Investing.com’s transcript summary. Mehrotra said customers were not looking to walk away and buying behavior had not changed despite the market’s swings. Its CHIPS Act restrictions expire on Dec. 9, after which excess cash goes back to shareholders.

Samsung made the same argument at a Morgan Stanley conference in Singapore. Kim Yong-kwan, president for strategy at its chip division, put neoclouds at about 8% of the market. “We do not expect these concerns to pose a significant threat to the broader AI growth trajectory,” he said, Reuters reported. Foundry sales head Kang Suk-chae said “today’s investment is not a result of speculation, but actual tangible demand,” and that the chip shortage would persist for the foreseeable future.

The stocks did not follow. Micron rose 3.2% before the bell, opened at $718.50, then closed down 1.00% at $705.27. Sandisk fell 2.64% to $776.22, its fifth straight loss, after a Zacks downgrade and a GF Securities warning of a NAND glut in 2027. Nvidia lost 2.89% to $150.96 on succession uncertainty, and the SOX ended up 0.10%. In Seoul the KOSPI opened 0.45% higher, fell as far as 4,963.61 and was down 0.12% at 5,061.34 at 14:33 KST. SK hynix was up 1.62% and Samsung down 0.62%, fnnews reported. Foreigners sold for a fifth straight session, Yonhap reported.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027. Unchanged. Producers say 2027 is sold and 2028 is being negotiated, and DDR5 spot edged up. But the stocks keep fading good news, and NAND, double-counted demand and 2028 capacity remain the weak spots. Cycle signal: contract books firm, shares unconvinced

HBM · DRAM supply · Contracts

HBM Will Take Nearly 30% of DRAM Wafers Next Year, Squeezing PC Supply, TrendForce Says

More detail from TrendForce’s Seoul roadshow, reported on Thursday by Seoul Economic Daily and BusinessKorea: HBM’s share of DRAM wafer input at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron rises to nearly 30% in 2027 from 23%. Because HBM uses three to four times as many wafers per bit, HBM bit supply grows 65.7% while PC DRAM bit supply falls 7.9%. CXMT adds only about 20%, to 36,000 wafers a month, though it plans 100,000–200,000 more after 2028. TrendForce sees cloud capex up 104% this year and 60% in 2027, to about $1.52 trillion, and still names 2028 as the turning point as new fabs arrive. Its weekly spot report says DRAM buyers are on the sidelines, with DDR4 1Gx8 down 1.43% to $44.25 (Nov 17), and NAND spot is now more than 20% below contract. On Thursday morning DDR5 16Gb rose 0.28% to $60.667. Separately, Google is reportedly moving to buy HBM directly for its TPUs instead of through Broadcom, Chosun Biz reported via TrendForce. An industry source said some long-term memory contracts already include price ceilings. And SK hynix is testing hybrid bonders from Hanwha Semitech and BESI for 20-high HBM5, due around 2028, Munhwa Ilbo reported. Reported
