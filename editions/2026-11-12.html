Memory Stocks Post Their Best Day in Months, Then Nvidia’s Leadership Crisis Clouds the Rally

CoreWeave’s results sent Micron up 11.9% and Sandisk up 13.7% on Wednesday. After the close, reports that Nvidia’s board was meeting to decide whether to remove Jensen Huang knocked Nvidia down 8–9% in extended trading. Seoul split on Thursday: Samsung rose, SK hynix slipped.

CoreWeave’s third-quarter beat set off the move. Chief executive Michael Intrator told CNBC that the company has 10 times as much demand as its remaining 2027 capacity, and that 2028 capacity is already oversubscribed several times over; it set 2026 capex at $37–39 billion (Yahoo Finance). Every SOX member rose (Reuters); Nvidia gained 2.87% in the session.

After the bell, The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia’s board was preparing to remove Huang. The New York Times reported a second relationship, with an executive who reported to him. CNBC and Reuters confirmed a board meeting on Wednesday night; no decision had been announced by Thursday morning. Nvidia reports on Nov. 17. Samsung and SK hynix are monitoring succession scenarios; one official said business talks could slow, while others said supply agreements were unlikely to be affected (Yonhap). In Seoul, the KOSPI closed at 5,342.73 (−0.61%), Samsung at ₩208,000 (+0.97%) and SK hynix at ₩1,254,000 (−1.80%); preliminary data show foreigners net bought about ₩347.8bn. DRAM spot was soft: DDR5 16Gb was flat at $60.233 and DDR4 16Gb fell 0.62% to $79.374, its largest drop of the week.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. Unchanged overall: enterprise a touch firmer, PC and consumer a touch looser. Cycle signal: enterprise firm, consumer softening

Prices · Enterprise vs consumer

Memory Makers Flag New Enterprise Price Hikes as PC Makers Sit on Inventory

Several leading memory makers have verbally told distributors they plan to raise prices for enterprise memory chips, and channel prices have already begun rising, Lanjing News reported, as summarized by TrendForce on Thursday. The increases are concentrated in DRAM contracts and enterprise SSDs, not consumer parts. In Shenzhen, 16GB DDR5 modules were quoted around RMB 1,600, below a reported RMB 2,000 average contract price for consumer modules, a price inversion. TrendForce said notebook shipments fell 15.8% q/q in 3Q26 and OEM DRAM inventory weeks have risen since then. It forecasts PC DRAM contract prices up 13–18% q/q in 4Q26, slower than last quarter, client SSDs below 5%, and enterprise SSDs up 15–20%. Suppliers tentatively plan to cut PC DRAM supply in 2027. Counterpoint: third-quarter DRAM revenue rose 369% y/y to just under $200 billion on shipments up about 4% (TechRadar).

HBM · Capacity

Samsung Breaks Ground on a ₩46 Trillion HBM Packaging Hub as Altman Warns of ‘Double-Counted’ Demand

Samsung broke ground on Line 5 at its Onyang campus, an advanced HBM packaging line due to start in June 2028, a year earlier than planned. It will invest ₩46 trillion there, ₩6 trillion for construction and ₩40 trillion for equipment installed in stages; Samsung says customers want more HBM than the 2027 volumes already agreed (Korea Times). SK hynix’s HBM4 yield has reportedly passed 70%; the company does not disclose yields (Chosunbiz). Reported OpenAI’s Sam Altman told about 50 Samsung affiliate presidents on Oct. 31 that “there has been double-counting of demand,” with only OpenAI and Anthropic driving major growth, so “there could be a supply shock and an unsettled period.” He still expects demand to grow enormously (Financial News, Nov 11). Kioxia plans to mass-produce 332-layer 10th-generation NAND as early as 2027 (Nikkei, via X).
