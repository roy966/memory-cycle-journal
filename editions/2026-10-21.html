Korea’s Chip Exports More Than Triple in Early October as Seoul’s Chipmakers Bounce Back

Customs data show record chip shipments for the first 20 days of the month. A blowout preliminary quarter from Supermicro sent Samsung and SK hynix up about 8% at one point before they gave back part of the gain.

Sources: Korea Customs Service via The Korea Times and trade.aljal.kr; Commercial Times via TrendForce; Chosun Biz; Supermicro; stockanalysis.com.

Korea’s chip exports rose 222.5% from a year earlier to $27.5 billion in the first 20 days of October, a record for the period, and made up 44.7% of all exports, the Korea Customs Service said. Total exports were a record $61.6 billion (The Korea Times). Per working day, chip exports averaged $2.12 billion, a little below the $2.22 billion of Oct. 1–10 and the $2.20 billion of Sept. 1–20 (customs data compiled by trade.aljal.kr).

The stocks bounced. After closing down 3.73% on Tuesday at 5,552.74, with Samsung off 2.98% and SK hynix 4.23% (Yonhap), the Kospi rose more than 5% on Wednesday morning, triggering the first buy-side program-trading halt since Aug. 20 (Seoul Economic Daily). By 13:00 the index was up 2.76% at 5,706.14, with Samsung up 5.08% and SK hynix 4.25%. Foreigners were net buyers of ₩423 billion while individuals sold ₩987 billion (Global Economic). The trigger came from the U.S.: after Tuesday’s close Supermicro said fiscal first-quarter revenue would be $18.5–19.5 billion, against guidance of $14.5–15.5 billion. Micron, down 0.98% to $896.07 in regular trading, rose 2.13% after hours.

Contract prices are still rising, but more slowly. Commercial Times reports institutional investors expect fourth-quarter contract prices up about 20% for DRAM and 30% for NAND, against roughly 80% in the second quarter and 40% in the third. It cites the high base, weaker end-market buying power and LTA price ceilings (TrendForce). That is above TrendForce’s own Sept. 30 guide of 10–15% for conventional DRAM and 15–20% for NAND. A Jefferies semis sales note called it “way more than buyside expects.”

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027, unchanged; a touch tighter on demand evidence, while the rate of price increase keeps slowing. Cycle signal: demand firm, ROC slowing

HBM · Market share

SK Hynix’s HBM4 Shipments Fall Short Again as Samsung Narrows the Gap

The industry believes SK hynix’s HBM4 shipments missed expectations in the third quarter, as they did in the second. Reasons cited are a partial delay to Nvidia’s Rubin and longer-than-expected tuning of mass-production parts (Chosun Biz). Third-quarter operating-profit consensus is ₩76.96 trillion (a 78.4% margin), down 5.6% from three months ago. Counterpoint puts SK hynix’s HBM revenue share at 50% in the second quarter, down from 58%, while Samsung rose to 33% from 21%. JPMorgan sees SK hynix’s share at 46% this year and 41% in 2027. UBS expects Samsung to pass SK hynix in 2027 HBM bits, 41% to 39%. Samsung’s HBM4 yield is believed to be about 80%. This moves share between suppliers more than it changes total demand. SK hynix reports on Oct. 28 and Samsung on Oct. 29. Cycle signal: share, not demand

Verdict
Late upcycle; tight through 2027, unchanged. A touch tighter: Korea’s chip exports more than tripled in early October, Supermicro beat its own guidance by about $4 billion, and DRAM lead times are still about 20 weeks. The rate of price increase keeps slowing, though: fourth-quarter DRAM contracts are seen up about 20%, half the third quarter’s gain, and spot is flat. SK hynix’s HBM4 slip moves share to Samsung but doesn’t change total demand. Seoul bounced on Wednesday after Tuesday’s rout.
