With markets shut, the debate moved to research notes: brokers see margins holding through 2028, while desks saw the heaviest tech selling since DeepSeek.

Bank of America says the memory selloff is “largely disconnected from memory fundamentals” (Seeking Alpha). SK hynix and Samsung are each down more than 50% from their June peaks but trade at 2–3× projected 2027–28 earnings, against 4–7× in previous upcycles. BofA sees 2028 EPS dipping only by a single-digit percentage and PC memory contract prices rising at least 15% a month (GuruFocus).

The tape has not agreed. Chipmakers fell an eighth straight day on Friday, the longest slide since 2008, and the Nasdaq 100 had its worst week since the April 2025 tariff turmoil. The 10-year yield was 5.09% (Bloomberg). Jefferies’ desk logged $5.5B of net tech selling this week, the most since DeepSeek in January 2025. It counts this as the seventh >9% drop in US semis since June, with positioning only mid-range. Jefferies’ Korea team calls the CXMT fear “overblown”: it puts the Samsung–CXMT DRAM process gap at 5–7 years and Samsung at a “historically low ~3x P/E.”

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027, unchanged. No new price prints this weekend; the one new data point was tighter (DDR4, below). Flows, not fundamentals, are moving the stocks.

Korea · Foreign selling

Who Is Selling Korea: Foreigners Have Dumped ₩176 Trillion of Samsung and SK hynix This Year, 87% of Their Total

Hyundai Motor Securities data cited by the Kyunghyang Shinmun: foreigners have net sold about ₩203T of Kospi stocks this year, and ₩176T (86.7%) of that is Samsung and SK hynix. Foreign ownership of Samsung fell to 46.38% on Oct 8, the lowest since January 2008. The pension fund has deferred rebalancing and the buybacks are ending. It sees foreign buying returning only after late-October earnings, the Nov 3 midterms and a mid-November Anthropic listing. On Friday foreigners net bought ₩342.8B of Kospi stocks. Samsung was their top buy, while SK hynix was their No. 2 sell (Yonhap).

China supply · CXMT

CXMT’s Real Threat Is Scale, Not 4F²: Capacity Could Top 600,000 Wafers a Month

On CXMT’s 4F² server DDR5 plan, a Hanyang University professor told the Korea Herald mass production this year is “practically impossible.” Samsung’s DS CTO said Samsung is working on 4F² too. The bigger risk is scale: new Shanghai and Hefei fabs could take CXMT above 600,000 wafers a month (Reuters). Its DRAM revenue share rose to 9.5% in Q2 from 7.6% (TrendForce). It has deals with Tencent (>$3B) and ByteDance (>$7B). TrendForce also found that revenue per wafer from 64GB DDR5 server modules passed HBM in Q1. CXMT reports Q3 on Oct 31. Cycle signal: 2028 supply

Legacy DRAM · Pricing

Old Chips, New Squeeze: DDR4 Contracts Up 24% in Three Months

Barclays, cited by Digital Daily: DDR4 contract prices are up 24% over three months versus 20% for DDR5, as legacy lines shrink to feed HBM. The paper also cites DRAM excess demand peaking at 13.9% in 4Q27 (source not named). Secondary The caution is low-end NAND: eMMC spot buyers expect “further price declines” (DRAMeXchange).
