TSMC Restates Foundry Focus After Friday’s 3D DRAM Report

A TSMC spokesperson told Tom’s Hardware the company “focuses on our core business at all times, which is dedicated IC foundry,” and that its mission is to remain “the trusted technology and capacity provider of the global logic IC industry.” Tom’s frames the remarks as a denial of Friday’s report that TSMC was charting 3D DRAM at Nvidia’s request. The quoted statement does not name 3D DRAM or Nvidia; the two accounts now conflict. Cash markets were closed. Spot DRAM is unchanged.

Specialty memory · Cycle color

Winbond Chairman Hopes Memory Prices Have Peaked

Winbond chairman Arthur Chiao (焦佑鈞) told reporters he does not think makers’ 70–80% gross margins can keep climbing — “consumers have no money” — and that he hopes prices have peaked. Only AI still absorbs higher prices; further inflation, he argued, would push phone and PC buyers to keep old devices another year. If the up-leg started in July 2025, a normal two-year rise would end around June 2027. From CSP conversations, he said 2027–28 data-center funding, land, power and buildings are already in place; he dates the next downcycle risk to 2029, when funding limits become the constraint. One specialty-memory chairman’s view, not a broker supply-demand table (TechNews, Nov 7).

Sell-side · Recirculation

Citi Still Sees 2027 DRAM Undersupply at −3.8%

A Nov 3 Citi note, recirculated on X over the weekend rather than newly initiated, still has 2027 DRAM undersupplied after Citi raised supply on higher bit-growth guidance from Samsung and SK hynix. The 2027 DRAM supply-demand gap is −3.8% (was −8.7%): supply +24% / demand +28%. NAND’s gap is −3.3% (was −6.1%): supply +22% / demand +26%. Citi raised 2027 capex-growth estimates to +55% DRAM / +35% NAND and put the current fulfillment ratio at about 70%. The note does not loosen a 2027 tightness call (Investing.com, Nov 3; chart relayed by @pequityresearch, Nov 8).
