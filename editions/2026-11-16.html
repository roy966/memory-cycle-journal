Seoul Dumps Its Memory Giants as NAND Spot Prices Crack and Nvidia’s Leadership Fight Spills Into the Weekend

SK hynix fell more than 7% and Samsung nearly 5% on Monday as foreign investors sold. Two things changed over the weekend: a Bank of America note showing NAND spot prices down about 20% since late October, and a public fight over whether Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia, a day before its results.

Korea’s memory stocks gave up an early bounce and fell hard on Monday. By 14:47 Seoul time SK hynix was down 7.61% at ₩1,123,500 and Samsung Electronics 4.85% lower at ₩196,000, the Seoul Economic Daily reported. SK Square, SK hynix’s parent, fell 10.10%. The KOSPI opened higher at 5,305.40, then slid through 5,200 and 5,100; it stood at 5,094.70, down 3.7%, at 14:13, Yonhap Infomax reported. Foreign investors sold a net ₩1.48 trillion on the main board and retail investors were the only net buyers. The paper tied the drop to the uncertainty after Huang’s exit and to “analysis showing NAND spot prices have entered a downtrend.”

The NAND analysis is a Bank of America note dated Nov 13, reported by GMT Eight and VLSI Korea. NAND spot prices are down about 20% since mid-to-late October. The 512Gb wafer is quoted at $14.3, down 12% on the week and 32% on the quarter, though still up 121% from a year ago, while the 16Gb DDR5 chip is flat on the week at $60.2. BofA expects NAND prices to fall by a low single-digit percentage in the first quarter of 2027, with PC and phone NAND down 5–10% and enterprise SSD flat to slightly up. Consensus expects a rise of 10% or more. It still assumes DRAM contract prices rise 8% this quarter and 5% in 1Q27. On VLSI Korea’s math, spot sits about 47% below the ~$27 October contract price, and spot usually leads contract by about a quarter.

Nvidia’s leadership fight adds to the nerves. The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Jensen Huang has discussed a return as CEO, with allies building a “war room” to lobby the board and seek White House support. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Fox News that Nvidia needs a “dramatic shakeup,” urged the board to look outside the company for its next chief executive, and asked for answers from the board and outside counsel within 10 days (CNBC). Nvidia reports on Tuesday.

Our call: late upcycle; tight through 2027 / into 2028. A touch looser overall: NAND spot is cracking while DRAM holds, and sentiment is weaker. HBM is still the scarce part. Cycle signal: NAND first to reprice; DRAM and HBM intact

Legal · DRAM

Judge Lets DRAM Price-Fixing Suit Against Samsung, SK hynix and Micron Go Ahead

A US federal judge denied a motion by Samsung, SK hynix and Micron to dismiss a proposed class action that accuses them of colluding to restrict DRAM supply, Tom’s Hardware reported. Judge Noël Wise rejected the argument that the case repeats a 2018 lawsuit, writing that the companies’ conduct was “more akin to hawks flying in formation.” The 17 plaintiffs cite four parallel moves: production cuts from October 2022 to April 2023, the pivot to HBM, DDR4 wind-down announcements within three months of each other, and a failure to add capacity as prices rose up to 500% in a year. The ruling does not find wrongdoing. The defendants must respond by Dec 7, and a case-management conference is set for Jan 12, 2027 (TrendForce).

HBM · Supply

SK hynix Adds HBM4 Capacity as Reports Say Nvidia’s Rubin Waits on HBM4

SK hynix is ordering more HBM bonding, cutting and test equipment for its Cheongju P&T6 plant, with installation targeted for the first half of 2027, the Electronic Times reported; at full operation P&T6 would add an estimated 20,000+ wafers a month of HBM capacity. The paper says demand from customers other than Nvidia is holding up. Separately, Taiwanese media report that Vera Rubin server volume has slipped from the end of this year to the first half of 2027 because there is not enough HBM4, with GB300 servers filling the gap. Nvidia has not confirmed that report Unconfirmed. Both reports say HBM4, not demand, is the constraint.

It expects “a moderation in the price.” Micron still expects its HBM share to match its DRAM share, with HBM outgrowing conventional DRAM through 2028. A move from 12-high to 8-high stacks would mean more HBM cubes, not less HBM. It says demand is tighter in 2027 and 2028. Jefferies Asia TMT sales note (Nov 16)

Its 3Q revenue beat was helped by pull-ins for specialty memory and controllers. Jefferies (Nov 16)
