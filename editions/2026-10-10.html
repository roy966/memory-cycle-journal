Samsung Signs Away Most of 2027 Before It Is Built

Long-term deals now cover an estimated 75–80% of next year's memory output, as an Apple order cut shows where the price pass-through stops

Samsung Electronics has secured long-term supply agreements covering about 75–80% of its planned memory production for next year, the Korea Economic Daily reported Friday, as Nvidia, Google and Microsoft seek to lock in supply for five years or more. SK hynix and Micron are also signing large long-term contracts, the report said. Samsung has not disclosed contract terms; the figure comes from industry sources.

How the deals work. On its July 30 call Samsung said it aimed to put 60–70% of capacity under multi-year contracts: a rolling five-year base renewed yearly, with deposit-like prepayments, about a quarter of which it had already received (Digital Today, TrendForce). It expects the shortage to deepen in 2027 and run into 2028, citing a 3.5-year lead time from new fab to wafers.

"Long-term agreements could leave manufacturers exposed when the cycle turns."— KED Global, deck of Oct 9 report

The tape. Micron closed Friday at $1,029.00, −0.66%, after the 4.79% drop on Thursday's OpenAI report. Korea and Taiwan were shut for holidays. Bloomberg's report that OpenAI expects ~$70 billion net annualized revenue by year-end took some of the funding scare out.

Why it matters for the cycle. Contracts with deposits turn a spot-driven business into a backlog. That lowers the odds of a 2027 price collapse. It does not fix the price: quarterly resets still reprice bits, and contract ceilings and floors decide who wears the next downturn.

Our call: late upcycle, tight through 2027. Slightly tighter than Oct 9 on volume visibility; consumer demand is the soft spot.

Demand

Apple Trims iPhone 18 Pro Orders as Memory Costs Bite

Apple told some suppliers to cut October component orders for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max by at least 15% vs its first request; one supplier said 15–20%, Nikkei Asia reported. The phones cost $100 more than last year's, a rise tied to memory costs. Chinese media later said Pro orders fell slightly while Pro Max rose slightly Unconfirmed.

Nvidia

Nvidia's Memory Bill Shows Up Off the Balance Sheet

SemiAnalysis says Nvidia's latest 10-Q disclosed $530B of gross off-balance-sheet obligations across six line items, up from $184B a quarter earlier, mainly supply commitments, "primarily commitments to buy memory." The largest buyer is pre-committing, the same story as the LTAs. Analyst view

Study Corner · Day 5 of 14

Peak vs trough, part 2: the language of the turn

Day 5 finishes the 2018-vs-2023 quote set. Re-read yesterday's lines side by side: peak talk was "fundamentally solid" demand (Samsung, 2017) and a market "structurally different" (Micron, 2018); trough talk was the "worst downturn in the last 13 years" (Micron, 2023) and cuts "by a meaningful level" (Samsung, 2023). Then compare with this week's language: "sold out through 2028" and five-year LTAs.

Quick questions on Day 4:

  1. Micron called the market "structurally different" in Oct 2018, near the peak. Which 2026 phrase plays that role now, and what would prove it wrong?
  2. Samsung in Oct 2018 blamed a coming slowdown on "seasonal effects." What is the 2026 equivalent excuse to watch for on the Oct 29 call?
  3. In 2023, production cuts marked the trough. With 75–80% of 2027 under LTA, what replaces production cuts as the adjustment tool in the next downturn: price floors, deposits, or capex?
