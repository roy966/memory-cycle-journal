Kioxia Shares Sink as Much as 17% After a Flat Profit Outlook

The flash maker expects December-quarter profit roughly level with September’s as NAND price gains slow. It blames shipment timing and says next year’s output is already sold out, but investors sold the slowdown.

What this shows: the NAND side of the cycle is slowing (Kioxia’s profit is flat quarter to quarter and its price gains have shrunk), while Korean DRAM stocks bounced as domestic pension funds and SK hynix’s own buyback absorbed selling. KOSPI is the close; other Korea figures are intraday at 13:50 KST (06:50 IST); US prices are Friday closes. Sources: Kioxia via Bloomberg, Nikkei and The Japan Times; Korea Exchange via Seoul Economic Daily; TrendForce.

Kioxia guided October–December operating profit to about ¥2 trillion, against a consensus near ¥2.35 trillion and ¥1.99 trillion in the September quarter, on revenue of ¥2.5 trillion (Bloomberg, Nikkei). Its NAND average selling price rose in the mid-20% range in the September quarter, after about 70% the quarter before (The Japan Times). The shares fell as much as 17% in Tokyo on Monday to ¥11,165, a six-and-a-half-month low (Nikkei), after an 11% drop on Germany’s Tradegate on Friday. Bernstein analysts led by Mark Li called the flat outlook “a massive miss.”

Management blamed timing rather than demand: orders were pulled into the September quarter, some enterprise-storage shipments slip to the March quarter, and more of the mix is lower-priced phone and PC parts. Chief financial officer Yoshihiko Kawamura said rapid growth returns in the March quarter and that AI data-center demand remains robust. Kioxia says its 2027 output is sold out, more than half of 2028 is allocated under long-term deals, and some customers are negotiating for 2029 and beyond (Bloomberg). The shares had surged more than 750% in the first half; Business Insider framed the drop as the AI trade getting pickier.

Our call: unchanged overall; NAND looser for a third session. Kioxia’s flat guide, Sandisk’s selloff and Nomura’s slower eSSD growth for 2027 point the same way. DRAM is steady. Cycle signal: NAND second derivative turning, DRAM steady

Markets · Korea

Korean Chip Stocks Rebound as Pension Funds and SK hynix’s Buyback Step In

The KOSPI opened 1.5% lower after weak Big Tech results, then reversed to trade 2.93% higher at 5,900.66 by 1:50 p.m. and closed at 5,859.08, up 2.24% (Yonhap). Pension funds bought ₩401.8 billion, and “other corporates”, a category believed to include SK hynix’s buyback, bought ₩386.8 billion (Seoul Economic Daily). SK hynix is buying back 14.92 million shares between Oct. 29 and Jan. 28 under its ₩100 trillion shareholder-return package. Separately, SK Square, SK hynix’s largest shareholder, pledged at least ₩4.3 trillion of returns, its largest since its 2021 launch, from more than ₩6.2 trillion of expected SK hynix dividends; it will return at least 60% of recurring dividend income through 2028, double its earlier 30% floor, plus all of the Kioxia-linked special dividend (Korea Herald).

Contracts · LTAs

Samsung and SK hynix Lock More Capacity Into Long-Term Orders

Samsung has signed long-term agreements with 10 customers and is negotiating with 10 more. It aims to put 60–70% of capacity under such contracts by 2030, as demand spreads from AI servers to mobile, auto and networking. SK hynix has deals with 14 customers, typically five years long, covering more than half of 2027–28 capacity. Samsung says volumes requested for extensions “far exceeded expectations” (Korea JoongAng Daily). TrendForce expects new fabs to lift DRAM supply significantly around 2028. At Friday’s close, Samsung was 39.9% and SK hynix 52.5% below their 52-week highs.

Supply · China

CXMT Pursues Long-Term Contracts and Says Its Prices Match Rivals’

China’s CXMT said long-term supply negotiations with several major customers are progressing well, and that its products are generally priced on par with those of overseas rivals. Its third-quarter gross margin rose 1.94 points to 89.53%, which it expects to hold in the fourth quarter (TrendForce, citing 21st Century Business Herald and Star Market Daily). Separately, Japan’s Shin-Etsu signalled that silicon-wafer demand now exceeds its long-term contracts: from January, some extra volume will ship at spot prices above contract levels, and capacity growth in 2027 is unlikely to top 10% (Commercial Times).
